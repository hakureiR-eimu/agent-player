**Yugoslavia** is a [formable](Formable countries.md) nation in the Balkans. It can be formed by any nation with a Jugoslav primary culture (Serbian, Croatian, Slovene, Bosniak, Bulgarian). ![Warning](/images/thumb/c/cb/Warning.png/24px-Warning.png) Bulgarian is kept as a primary culture *only* if the country is formed by [Bulgaria](Bulgaria.md).

Most of its core states start under the control of [Austria](Austria.md) and the [Ottoman Empire](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Turkey). Yugoslavia can also form [Central Europe](Central Europe.md), by virtue of Slovene being a Central European culture.[1]

## Historical background

The Slavic parts of central and southern Europe were long under control of non-Slavic empires, particularly the Austrians and Ottomans. The concept of a unified South Slavic country began to take hold in the 19th century, especially as the Ottoman Empire weakened. However, the realization of Yugoslavia would not come until the end of World War I, when the break-up of the Austro-Hungarian Empire allowed its Slavic territories to join with the Kingdom of Serbia as Yugoslavia.

## Formation

Nine of the 12 core states are needed in order to form Yugoslavia
Yugoslavia can be formed by the following nations when they control at least nine of its 12 core states. As Yugoslavia is a [major unification](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Major_unification), it can be formed without holding the required states directly. Instead, a *unification candidate* can gain the support of other Yugoslav countries. The candidate must be at least a major power, as long as there are no higher-rank candidates.

Bulgarian remains as a primary culture only if the country is formed by [Bulgaria](Bulgaria.md).

[Bulgaria](Bulgaria.md) is disallowed from forming Yugoslavia unless it chooses to pursue the Yugoslav idea after completing the Prussia of the Balkans journal entry, which requires the National Awakening DLC.

### Core states

Yugoslavia has 12 core states, comprising the northern part of the Balkans strategic region plus the states Istria, Banat, and Vojvodina. These comprise the homelands of its primary cultures.

Yugoslavia's core states contain a large amount of coal and smaller amounts of iron and lead, but no sulfur. Several of its states have the Terra Rossa trait, increasing vineyard throughput and wine output.

## Strategies and guides

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Yugoslavia
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

### Serbia

[Serbia](Serbia.md) starts in a rough situation, which is troubling as they are the primary contender to form a nation of all Slavs. They have no industry besides a small deal of agriculture, with only 10 starting brigades, only owning their capital state. Serbia possesses cores on unowned portions of Western Serbia and Eastern Serbia as well as Vojvodina, as well as having a number of historical Yugoslav lands as homelands of Serbian culture. The entire population is Serbian and Orthodox, which pairs well with the starting [laws](Laws.md) *National Supremacy* and *State Religion*. As Serbia expands, the player will have to choose between oppressing the Turkish, Albanian, Hungarian, and other minorities who are discriminated against at start, or to change the laws of Serbia to extend rights and prevent radicalism.

Serbia has some historical characters, but other interest group leaders are randomly generated. A player can use this to their advantage, by restarting the game until a set up is found with the desired character combination. A suggestion would be a Jingoist Landowner, a Republican Armed Forces, and an Abolitionist Orthodox Church. In this set up, laws like Professional Army and Colonial Resettlement can be passed to gain approval by the Landowners, while the Armed Forces and Church can be used to begin abolishing Serfdom and weakening the power of the Landowners.

Perhaps most pressing, you begin as a protectorate of the Ottoman Empire. Addressing this insult will be the primary diversion point for the player’s strategy, as how one goes about acquiring independence and freedom from Turkish hands will completely change the steps needed to succeed with any given strategy. To form Yugoslavia, Serbia needs land from both the Ottomans and Austria,

One strategy is for the player to be patient and wait until 1866, building your country up and biding your time. January 1st, 1866 the Ottoman Empire will become an unrecognized major power – unless the AI completes the *Sick Man of Europe* journal entry, and all subjects they have will suddenly become independent, with all the benefits and issues that brings.

It is absolutely crucial that players have adequately prepared their market and industry before this happens. A player who builds a Serbia with a large manufacturing sector, but no agriculture, will suddenly be swarmed by angry mobs and radicalism when the Ottoman wheat is closed off to Serbian consumers.

A more "active" method begins at day 1, where the entire focus is to look for the first opening to jump the Ottomans with a Serbian play for independence and southern Serbia, and to bring either Austria or Russia in as a Great Power backer. In this case, the player will post-independence build their economy from the ground up, and will need a solid understanding of production and consumption, as well as pop growth and vertical integration, to truly make a nation of all Slavs a great nation.

### As a Great Power subject

Players of Great Powers with non-European heritage primary cultures can consider having Yugoslavia as a subject. Liberating [Croatia](Croatia.md) (and annexing Venetia and Istria) will turn Austria into a landlocked country, greatly weakening it, while the Ottoman Empire is usually weak as well.
