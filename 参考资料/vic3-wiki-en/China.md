*"The Qing Dynasty has ruled in China for almost two hundred years and controls a third of the world's population, after the apex of its power under the Qianlong Emperor. However, the spread of opium and the interference of the European powers threatens the Dynasty’s period of internal balance. What will the future of China look like?"*

**China** – called *Great Qing* at the start of the game – is unrecognized major power in East Asia. It boasts the largest population in the entire world - more than tripling the numbers of the next largest contender - and the highest nominal Prestige, but the Empire's power is grealty hindered due to its technological and economic underdevelopment, making it, in practice, a paper tiger. If China manages to overcome its crises and powerful rulers steer the country in the right direction, however, it will be poised to become the world's greatest power. However, in order to do so, the Middle Kingdom will have to contend with the imperial ambitions of the Western Powers - as well as potentially those of [Japan](Japan.md).

Its Capital is the city of Beijing in the homonymous state, but the market capital is the city of Guangzhou in the state of Western Guangdong. This is due to the existence of the Canton System, preventing trade through other ports.

China is one of four recommended countries for the *Egalitarian Society* [objective](Objective.md), the others being [France](France.md), [Russia](Russia.md), and [Mexico](Mexico.md).

## Historical background

The region of China was ruled by many different dynasties across history. In 1836, China was ruled by the Qing dynasty – a Manchu dynasty that was proclaimed in 1636 The Qing entered Beijing in 1644 (during the reign of the Shunzhi Emperor) and consolidated rule over China and Taiwan by 1683 (during the reign of the Kangxi Emperor). Between 1636 and 1836, the Qing emperors had extended the rule of China to the north and west, conquering Mongolia and Dzungaria.

The 1800s saw many setbacks for China and the Qing dynasty. European colonial powers defeated China in several wars and imposed harsh "unequal treaties". These defeats also encouraged internal unrest and China had several rebellions during the 1800s and early 1900s, including the Taiping Rebellion and Boxer Rebellion. The Qing dynasty was eventually overthrown in the 1911 Revolution which established the Republic of China.

The early Republic of China was unstable as the first president, Sun Yat-sen, handed over authority to Yuan Shikai, leader of the modernized Beiyang Army. Yuan consolidated his power over the Republic and established military governors of the provinces. At the end of 1915, Yuan proclaimed himself Emperor of China, but he quickly abdicated in the face of overwhelming opposition and died soon after. Following his death, the military governors effectively ruled their provinces independently and fought each other over the next two decades in the Warlord Era.

### Interest groups

China starts with the Scholar-Officials in government. The Armed Forces, Rural Folk, Confucian Scholars, and Literati are in opposition, while the Trade Unions, Petite Bourgeoisie, and Industrialists are marginalized.

The Confucian Scholars have the Confucian ideology instead of the default Moralist and Pious ideologies. This makes them ignore Education and Health System laws, but be more receptive of the starting Freedom of Conscience. Meanwhile, the Literati lose the Anti-Clerical ideology. The Scholar-Officials have the Scholar-Gentry instead of the default Paternalistic, which removes their opinion on economic matters and makes them endorse Appointed Bureaucrats. Finally, the Petite Bourgeoisie have the Mercantile ideology causing them to endorse economic and trade reforms.

### Diplomacy

China has three subjects at game start: vassal [Tibet](Tibet.md), and tributaries [Lanfang](Lanfang.md) and [Joseon](Korea.md).

China's starting interests are: hegemonic (5) in North China and South China, influential (3) in Himalayas, Central Asia, Siberia, and Northeast Asia, and observant (1) in Indonesia.

China starts with a claim on Sakhalin, and as [Russia](Russia.md) and [Japan](Japan.md) also start with a claim on the state, it is likely to be a source of tension between the three countries.

China has a diplomatic treaty with the [Japanese Shogunate](Japan.md): *Nagasaki China Trade*, which gives China 20 units of lead in exchange for 20 units of tools, binding for 18 years from game start. There is also the *Luso-Chinese Agreement* with [Portugal](Portugal.md): the European country transfers 150 to China each week in exchange for a treaty port in Macau (Western Guangdong). Uniquely, this agreement is bound for 99 years, practically the entire game.

Aside from its subjects [Tibet](Tibet.md), [Joseon](Korea.md) and [Portuguese](Portugal.md) Macau, China also neighbors [Russia](Russia.md), [Dai Nam](Dai Nam.md), the decentralized nation of [Sip Song Chau Tai](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Sip_Song_Chau_Tai), [Luang Prabang](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Luang_Prabang), [Burma](Burma.md), [Ladakh](Ladakh.md), [Chitral](Chitral.md), [Kunduz](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Kunduz), [Kokand](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Kokand), and the Kazakhs [Orta Zhuz](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Orta_Zhuz) and [Uly Zhuz](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Uly_Zhuz).

## Strategy and guides

| ![Ambox outdated info.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/6/65/Ambox_outdated_info.png/22px-Ambox_outdated_info.png) | Please help with verifying or updating this guide. It was last verified for version 1.9. |
|---|---|

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
China
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

### Laws

China's Landowner IG, the Scholar-Officials, do not oppose changing out of Traditionalism (Economic System) due to their "Scholar-Gentry" trait. Therefore, China can attempt to abandon Traditionalism after building enough Bureaucracy capacity. Like most other Landowner IGs, the Scholar-Officials do not oppose Landed Voting, so passing the law for a lower government ideology penalty should be considered. The protestant missions and Heavenly Kingdom events impose a steep price for repealing Closed Borders, which in turn prevents invitation of agitators for the purpose of starting desired movements.

### Economy

China's population is the world's largest by far, and this will lead to the largest economy if you play your cards right. The nation is vast and contains ample amounts of every resource you will need to become a manufacturing juggernaut with the notable exception of rubber, but this is easily accessible from nearby regions or trade. Although you're technologically behind the European powers [in all matters](China.md), including in production methods, you make up for it through sheer numbers. For the same reason, China can afford to offer investment rights liberally without fear of domestic investors being crowded out of a limited domestic market.

However, China's great strength is also its greatest problem: building up the economy fast enough to satisfy increasing standard of living expectations demands relentless focus on investment. [Bureaucracy](Capacity.md) requirements for institutions are steep, and the most populous states will have deep tax capacity penalties for most if not all of the century. If insufficient [SoL](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/SoL) leads to radicals, who then cause [turmoil](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Turmoil), which then compounds tax collection penalties, China's budget may collapse. The cost of a welfare institution will be immense, but reduced turmoil may cover the expense in the medium term, if China prepares sufficient reserves in advance. China is obviously not unique in these matters, but the scale of the challenges certainly are. Make sure to expand your raw resource base *before* you start increasing the size of your manufacturing, which in turn come *before* you build more offices and barracks.

China starts with three other countries in its market through their subject-overlord relationship : [Korea](Korea.md), [Tibet](Tibet.md), and [Lanfang](Lanfang.md), a small Hakka Chinese republic in south-east Asia. These economies are obviously dwarfed by China's own, but, the might of the Imperial Army and the bounty of your economy means that many powerful nations will soon come knocking at your door for trade and diplomatic relations. While Canton System isn't complete isolationism, only limiting trade to 1 state rather than completely, the **-25%** debuff to Trade Capacity and the limit of 50 trade centers in the state severely hinder your trade potential.

### Diplomacy

Although China will have reduced relations with [Great Britain](Great Britain.md) due to the opium ban, granting Britain Trade Privileges before banning opium may please them enough not to declare war. In addition, improving relations with Britain will help with gaining recognition, as China's most viable Great Power target is [Russia](Russia.md), especially after Russia gains claims on Chinese territory. After the opium ban is successful, consider granting Britain Investment Rights.

### Military

China starts off with the world's largest military in terms of manpower, with 485 regulars, it is impressive on paper but it is incapable in reality. The imperial army at the start, though massive, is actually a relatively feeble fighting force. China only begins with 4 army and 3 navy [techs](Military technology.md) unlocked, meaning that their regular battalions are composed of irregular infantry and hussars and there are no artillery battalions. In addition to the fact that at the start of the game the widespread opium addictions modifier will halve the already poor offence and defence. The navy is incredibly small for an empire the size of the Qing, another hindrance to fighting the other powers. This means that from the start, you are sure to be defeated by the recognised European powers, unless you have one of the Great Powers as your ally. They also start with the Peasant Levies army model.

Before the European powers can be fought on equal footing, you'll have to focus your initial tech investments on the army. The [First Opium War](Chinese content.md) is a conflict you are sure to lose alone as there is insufficient time to strengthen your banner armies to stand up to the modern British army. If you are playing on lenient AI, the best course of action is to improve relations with Russia and get a defensive pact; with their more modern army, at least compared to your own, that is not affected by opium, you stand a pretty good chance of resisting the British naval invasions in Beijing and other places. To actually win the war, you should use your small navy to land small forces in the undefended colonies of the British to gain war exhaustion, like the Gambia or British Guiana. In standard or harsh you are unlikely to get alliances, so just guerrilla warfare on the British colonies and try to keep Beijing free from redcoats if you can.

An alternative is to start a [diplomatic play](Diplomatic play.md) in India (or any region where Great Britain is sure to intervene) and back down once Great Britain has joined. This gives China a 5-year truce which is enough to complete the opium ban.

Once the First Opium War concludes (or the opium ban has been successfully enforced), you can set sights on modernising the archaic banner armies. The first techs you should get are Line Infantry and Napoleonic Warfare for mobile artillery. With these two techs your military will be on much more equal footing with the Great Powers. With a couple dozen small arms and artillery industries and some imports if you need, you can upgrade all your regulars to line infantry and get 50-60 battalions of mobile artillery to split between your armies. With the sheer numbers in your army (around 600 battalions) you can bully any of your smaller neighbours and challenge individual Great Powers for recognition or colonies. By around the 70s mark, you should be looking into getting General Staff for skirmisher infantry and Triage, and later get Breech-Loading Artillery for shrapnel artillery, which your industry at this point in the game should be able to supply. As long as you have the same major army techs like infantry and artillery upgrades your army will be more than capable of taking on the other Great Powers, as recovery and kill rate and such can be ignored as long as you have overwhelming numbers. As a general rule, it's very inexpensive to supply your armies with chocolate and liquor as supplements to help boost morale, as sugar and liquor are usually very abundant in the Qing market.

On the aforementioned navy, though it is laughably small considering the country it serves, that's something that can and likely should be fixed. Though it may be tempting to neglect it and go all in on the army, it goes without saying that attempting to expand into the East Indies, Japan, or even Africa without a decent sized navy is all but impossible no matter how big your army is. China has a large coastline, and many coastal states, so with your industrial output you can at least build a naval force large enough to land your army and keep them from getting cut off from supplies far away from the mainland.

### Expansion

As it did in real life, the Chinese empire in-game has most of what it needs inside its own borders, and doesn't really need to expand much further. However, for a more ambitious ruler, even this isn't enough. At the start of the game, it's clear that the sharks have already started to circle, with the Russians in the north and west, the British to the southwest, and even the Dutch in the southeast. With these neighbors, lanes for expansion seem a bit narrow, but there are lands in Central Asia and in Indochina that can easily be [puppeted](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Puppet) or conquered even in the early game. However, be aware that throwing your weight around this early is likely to provoke conflict with the two powers to your north and south, one which you are unlikely to win in the first few decades into the game. Even without modernising, reclaiming Macau from [Portugal](Portugal.md) (even with Great Britain as Portugal's ally) is possible. To the east, [Joseon](Korea.md) and [Japan](Japan.md) can be juicy targets too, but as the Shogunate is an island country and your navy is likely pitiful, this isn't really an option early on. Conversely, Joseon is already a tributary at the start of the game, and will usually accept a reduction in autonomy to become a vassal. Over at Borneo, having Lanfang as a vassal would facilitate the conquest of the island.

Thus, it is normally better to wait until the mid-game, when your military and its supporting industries are large and advanced enough to actually go toe-to-toe with the British and the Russians if need be. Once ready, the world is China's oyster and you'll be able to go any way you wish.

#### Macau

Macau can be reclaimed from Portugal in a number of ways:

- If recognized, China can re-negotiate to remove the relevant clause, or withdraw from the treaty;
- If still unrecognized, China can use the *Liberate Country* wargoal

It's best to wait until researching Pan-nationalism to reclaim Macau, as doing so early may prevent the Boxer Rebellion JE from firing, preventing completion of the Fragile Unity JE. If the player chooses to simply reconquer it from Portugal, the war should be very easy; Portugal has a small army and will have great difficulty transporting it across the globe.

### Power Bloc

China's vast population makes joining a [power bloc](Power bloc.md) impossible, but China easily qualifies to lead a bloc once Canton System is repealed. While founding the bloc comes at a substantial cost to relations with the great powers, the power bloc idea bonuses are well worth it, though take care to patch up relations with at least one great power quickly for the sake of recognition.
