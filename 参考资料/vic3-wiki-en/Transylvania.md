*"Separated from Hungary following Rákóczi's War in 1711, the Principality of Transylvania finds itself at the center of German, Hungarian, and Romanian attention. Can Transylvania maintain its autonomy, or will it be swallowed up by its ambitious neighbors?"*

**Transylvania** is a minor power in Eastern Europe. It begins the game as a Crown Land of [Austria](Austria.md).

Transylvania starts with its capital in Northern Transylvania (Kolozsvár), rather than its defined capital state of Southern Transylvania.

Transylvania can form the [Danubian State](Danubian State.md) or [Germany](Germany.md).

## Historical background

In the 13th century, Transylvania formed the eastern frontier of the Kingdom of Hungary, administered as an autonomous voivodeship. Its ethnic and cultural origins have long been debated. According to Encyclopedia Britannica, scholars traditionally present two major scenarios regarding the continuity of the region's Romanian-speaking population: one posits a full withdrawal of Romanized populations after the Roman departure from Dacia, while the other argues for the uninterrupted survival of a Romance-speaking, Romanized Dacian population. Britannica notes that nationalist historians deploy one or the other scenario to justify modern territorial claims or claims to indigeneity, but that *"most scholars who are not nationally affiliated assume the second scenario"*. This ongoing debate has shaped perceptions of Transylvania’s ethnic history well into the modern era with mainly Romanian historians assuming a Romanian majority and mainly Hungarian historians assuming a Hungarian majority at the eve of the Mongol Invasion. During the late medieval period, Transylvania functioned as an autonomous province within the Hungarian Crown, structured around the three privileged nations: the Hungarians (Magyars), the Székely, and the Saxons (Germans), each with its own estates and legal privileges. Romanians, though forming a substantial share of the rural population, were officially designated as tolerated population and lacked the political rights afforded to the three privileged nations. This system, solidified between the 14th and 16th centuries, defined Transylvanian local governance and estate representation for centuries.

Following the Battle of Mohács (1526) and the collapse of the medieval Kingdom of Hungary under Ottoman pressure, Transylvania emerged as a semi-independent principality, nominally under Ottoman suzerainty but maintaining its own diplomatic agency. Throughout the 16th and 17th centuries, it acted as a buffer state between the Ottoman Empire and the Habsburg lands while cultivating its own political culture, religious pluralism, and economic autonomy. By the end of the 17th century, Transylvania was gradually incorporated into the Habsburg Monarchy, a process formalized after the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699). Under Austrian rule, the principality’s autonomy was reduced, and Habsburg administrators introduced new political, religious, and military structures. Although the ethnic balances in earlier centuries are impossible to determine with certainty, Austrian statistics from 1730 list the population at roughly 725,000 consisting of about 57.9% Romanians, 26.2% Hungarians and 15.1% Germans. While the first modern census in 1850 recorded 2,073,372 inhabitants out of which 59.1% were Romanians, 25.9% Hungarians and 9.3% Germans, figures that would later play an important role in 19th and 20th century national movements.

During the Revolutions of 1848, Transylvania became a battleground between competing national projects. Hungarian revolutionaries sought the unification of Transylvania with Hungary and the abolition of Habsburg absolutism, while Romanian leaders opposed incorporation into a revolutionary Hungary that did not recognize Romanian national rights. The result was a Romanian counter-movement that aligned with the Habsburgs, leading to violent clashes between the Hungarian and Romanian militia across the region. After the Habsburgs suppressed the Hungarian revolution, Transylvania briefly regained its separate administration. However, the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 fundamentally altered this arrangement: it established the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary and transferred Transylvania into the Kingdom of Hungary proper, ending its distinct political institutions and accelerating Magyarization policies. The early 20th century brought growing national tensions, culminating in World War I. As Austria-Hungary collapsed in 1918, Transylvania's national communities issued separate declarations regarding their future. On 1 December 1918, the Great National Assembly at Alba Iulia, representing the Romanian majority, proclaimed the union of Transylvania with Romania. The region's German representatives also issued a declaration in early 1919 supporting the union, though the Hungarian leaders rejected it. These events set the stage for the post-war settlement, and the Treaty of Trianon in 1920 that internationally recognized Transylvania as part of the Kingdom of Romania, reshaping the political map of Central Europe.

## Formation

If Transylvania ceases to exist, it can be [released](Releasable countries.md) by a country controlling its capital state of Southern Transylvania. Its core states include Southern Transylvania and Northern Transylvania.

If Transylvania ceases to exist and is then released, it will also have the Romanian primary culture in addition to its starting list of cultures, and will be able to form [Romania](Romania.md).

## Scripted content

If Transylvania is independent, [Wallachia](Wallachia.md) or [Moldavia](Moldavia.md) can get a personal union through the Journal Entries [Unite the princinpalities and All for one](Romania.md).

### Interest groups

Transylvania begins with the Magnates in government. The Armed Forces, Catholic Church, Intelligentsia, Kleinbürger, and Landvolk are in opposition, while the Industrialists and Trade Unions are marginalized.

## Military

Transylvania has the Professional Army law.

Transylvania starts with 19 battalions and can call upon 8 conscripts. All Barracks use No Organized Training. The battalions compose a single army.

| Army name | Battalions | Generals | HQ region |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generalkommando Hermannstadt | 16 Line Infantry 3 Hussars | Ferdinand Karl von Österreich-Este | Balkans |

As a landlocked country, Transylvania has no flotillas.
