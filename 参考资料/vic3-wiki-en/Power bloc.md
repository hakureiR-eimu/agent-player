**Power blocs** represent international organizations, associations, and empires. Each power bloc has a *central identity* (type) and a number of *principles* that grant benefits to the leader and, usually, to other members.

## Creating a power bloc

Only great powers, major powers, and unrecognized major powers can create a power bloc. Creating a power bloc requires **500** influence, and the country that creates the power bloc becomes its leader. Creating a power bloc causes relations to decrease by **30** with other power bloc leaders. If another country becomes the leader of the bloc, the influence cost shifts to them and the former leader no longer pays it.

A country with Isolationism or Canton System cannot create a Power Bloc. There are no further requirements for creating Trade Leagues or Ideological Unions. However, countries with National Militia cannot create a Military Treaty, countries with Multiculturalism cannot create a Cultural Commonwealth, only countries with State Religion or Freedom of Conscience can create a Religious Convocation, and only countries with Monarchy or Social Monarchy can create a Sovereign Empire. However, these restrictions do not apply after forming a power bloc; for example a country with a Sovereign Empire power bloc can later abolish the monarchy without losing their bloc. Subjects of all kinds join and leave power blocs with their overlord and cannot form power blocs.

When creating a new power bloc, the first mandate – which is given for free – must be used to select one of the two primary principles for the central identity.

Additionally, with the Sphere of Influence DLC, the power bloc's name, emblem, and statue can be customized, including a random option for the name and statue. Without Sphere of Influence, the name can be rerolled, but otherwise no customization can be used.

Principles represent lesser aspects of a power bloc. Each central identity has two primary principles, with one primary principle restricted to that identity. A power bloc that does not have at least one primary principle for its central identity selected suffers a cohesion penalty.

If the Sphere of Influence DLC is enabled, up to four principles can be selected, with the third and fourth slots requiring both a high power bloc rank and certain number of members. Without the DLC, only one principle can be used. Once a slot is filled, it can continue to be used even if the unlocking conditions are later false.

Each principle has three tiers and enacting a principle requires a number of *mandates* equal to the principle tier; upgrading a principle costs the difference in tiers. Up to three mandates can be stored for use, so it is possible to enact a tier three mandate immediately, or to upgrade it tier by tier. Principles can be changed out at any time, as long as the mandate cost can be paid.

### Mandate progress

| Non-leader member rank |  |
|---|---|

| Major power | **+5** |
| Unrecognized major power | **+2** |

Mandate progress is gained weekly based on the number and rank of non-leader members in a power bloc. Higher rank members give more mandate; insignificant and unrecognized powers do not give any mandate progress. Mandate progress also increases naturally based on the prestige rank of the power bloc, with a maximum of **+5** for the Rank 1 power bloc, decreasing to **+4** for the Rank 2 power bloc and so on to a minimum of **+1** mandate progress for any rank power bloc. The total is also multiplied by the current cohesion level. Reaching 2000 progress grants the power bloc leader a new mandate to select, upgrade, or change a principle.

## Cohesion

Cohesion is a value between **0** and **100** that represents how well a power bloc functions and is generally reduced by including more members. Each cohesion level applies various effects to the power bloc and its members and certain bloc leader actions require a high enough level of cohesion to use.

| Level | Cohesion value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Fractured | 0-19 | −50% Leverage generation −60% Mandate progress |
| Divided | 20-39 | −20% Leverage generation −30% Mandate progress |
| Stable | 40-59 | **−10%** Mandate progress |
| Controlled | 60-79 | +10% Leverage generation +10% Mandate progress |
| Orchestrated | 80-100 | +20% Leverage generation +25% Mandate progress |

Cohesion can change by up to **±1** per week, from the current amount towards the target amount.

## Leverage

Leverage represents the amount of influence a bloc has over a given country. Each country has a pool of 1000 leverage that is divided between itself and all power blocs based on each one's *leverage share*. A power bloc needs at least 200 more leverage than any other power bloc or the country in order to invite the country to the bloc.

Leverage share is based on the sum of *leverage factors* of all power blocs generating leverage on a country plus the country's own leverage resistance. Leverage resistance is determined by country ranking, population, and certain laws, such as trade policy, free speech, and migration. Leverage factors are not capped, but each bloc or the country's share is the percentage of the total, so leverage share always sums to 100%.

For example if a country has 1000 out of 4000 total Leverage Factor their Leverage Share is 25%. This percentage is then applied to the pool of 1000 Leverage resulting in 250 leverage, or 25% of 1000. This final leverage number must be at least **+200** greater than the next highest leverage in order to invite the country to the Power Bloc. In this case the country could be invited if the next highest leverage was no greater than 50.

### Joining and leaving a power bloc

When inviting countries to join a power bloc, a country must have enough leverage and furthermore the country has to accept joining. Acceptance depends on various factors such as [relations](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Relations), [attitude](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Attitude), difference between [rank](Rank.md), cohesion and the type of power bloc. An obligation can be offered to sway a country to join. If the invitation is rejected, relations deteriorate by **50**.

To leave a power bloc, the leaving country must be independent and the power bloc must have 30 leverage or less. Furthermore it is not possible to leave a power bloc less than 6 months after joining.

It is also possible that the power bloc leader decides to unilaterally remove a member. Doing so deteriorates relations by **50**, creates a 12 months [truce](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Truce) between the countries and lowers cohesion of the power bloc by **10%**.

## Power struggle

A *power struggle* starts if any independent power bloc member has 20% more prestige than the current leader. If the contender can maintain at least 15% more prestige for 12 months, they become the new leader of the power bloc. If the original founder is the contender, they only need to maintain more prestige for 9 months.

A power bloc statue provides 20 urbanization and uses 1 infrastructure.
