**Philippines** is a minor power and [Spanish](Spain.md) colony in the Indonesia region. It is a recognized state in Asia, one among very few.

## Historical background

The Philippines is an archipelago that comprises 7,641 islands with a total land area of 300,000 square kilometers (115,831 sq mi). It is the world's fifth largest island country. The eleven largest islands contain 95% of the total land area. It is divided into three island groups: *Luzon*, the *Visayas*, and *Mindanao*. The Philippine archipelago lies in Southeast Asia, and numbers some 7,641 islands. The largest of these islands is Luzon at about 105,000 square kilometers (40,541 sq mi). The next largest island is Mindanao at about 95,000 square kilometers (36,680 sq mi). The archipelago is around 800 kilometers (500 mi) from the Asian mainland and is located between Taiwan and Borneo.

## Scripted content

Main article:
Philippine content
Philippines has access to a number of journal entries and events, particularly with the [Iberian Twilight](Iberian Twilight.md) immersion pack enabled. These include:

- **Perla del Mar de Oriente** — a journal entry covering the growing national consciousness and political tensions in the Philippines, with outcomes leading to either Philippine independence or continued reliance on [Spain](Spain.md).
- **Archipelagic Ethnogenesis** — a journal entry covering the formation of a unified Filipino national identity, resulting in either a distinct Filipino culture or a Hispanophone Filipino culture.
- **Advancements in the Archipelago** — a journal entry covering the economic development of the archipelago through agriculture and port construction.
- **Rebuilding the Manila Cathedral** — a journal entry covering the reconstruction of the Manila Cathedral following an earthquake.

For full details on all journal entries, events, and their effects, see [Philippine content](Philippine content.md).

## Strategy and guides

| ![Ambox outdated info.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/6/65/Ambox_outdated_info.png/22px-Ambox_outdated_info.png) | Please help with verifying or updating this guide. It was last verified for version 1.12. |

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Philippines
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.
Early in the game, the Philippines will have to decide if they will remain loyal to the [Spanish](Spain.md) crown or not. Spain is a natural protector, and [Great Britain](Great Britain.md), [China](China.md) or the [East Indies](East Indies.md) can potentially be threatening to an independent Philippines. Furthermore, Spain already has a large market, where Philippines can specialise in a few crucial industries, especially shipyards. On the other hand, independence opens up more diplomatic possibilities to conquer states in Borneo, South East Asia or (later) China. The independence war itself is relatively simple if no Great Powers support Spain, as Spain has a weak navy, incapable of supporting its army in naval invasions.

From the start of the game, the states of Mindenao and Visayas are not integrated, and they hold sizeable portions of Phillipines' population, so integrating them as soon as possible is a must. By switching to a better PM, the starting administration buildings is enough for now, but there is lacking paper, so a paper mill and a logging camp to supply it is a good choice for the first two buildings.

### Colonization

Philippines' Laws are actually decent to start with. Mindenao is only partially colonized, and therefore frontier colonization is a good choice for a first law, as there is no actual resistance to implementing it.

### Primary cultures

The Philippines starts with very low [acceptance](Acceptance.md) score of the various indigenous groups that make up the majority of its population, and is only able to function thanks to its Subjecthood citizenship law that raises acceptance status for pops living in their homelands. Moving to National Supremacy or Racial Segregation will reduce their de facto acceptance by removing, whereas Cultural Exclusion will penalize the non-hispanophone pops while benefiting Filipino Mestizos.

Upon researching Nationalism, the interrelated Archipelagic Ethnogenesis and Perla del Mar de Oriente Journal Entries fire if the Philippines is still a subject of Spain. Completion of Perla requires resolving the question of the Philippines' relations to Spain, either improving relations and becoming a higher rank of subject, or successfully winning independence. Either way, the resulting events will upgrade Filipino Mestizos to primary culture status, opening the road for acceptance of all of the Insulindian heritage pops through the more typical liberalization of citizenship laws, leading to the conclusion of the Archipelagic Ethnogenesis journal entry. This, in turn, will lead to the transformation of the Filipino Mestizo culture to either Filipino or Pilipino, depending on whether the Philippines continues to be a subject of Spain, and adds large assimilation bonuses to the Visayas, Mindanao and Luzón.
