## General overview

### Economy

[Japan](Japan.md) starts out with a sizable population but with minimal industry. It has no tooling workshops, no mines except one level of lead mine and no steel mills, limited logging camps, paper mills and shipyards, no universities or arts academies, and no military industries. It doesn't even have a glass workshop to make use of its sole Lead Mine. Japan does have sizeable Silk Plantations and the unique technology Sericulture, boosting production. Japan does a few clothing workshops, but is mostly exporting the silk to the [Netherlands](Netherlands.md).

As of [version 1.12](Patch 1.12.md) a serious early to mid-game challenge for Japan is unemployment. Even with monopolies on low-employment plantations to prevent the private sector from displacing too many peasants, population growth will create large numbers of jobless people who quickly radicalize, and the player must learn to live with them. Resource buildings, in particular the low-cost Logging Camp and Fishing Wharf, will mitigate the problem while the slots last.

### Politics

Its main and most powerful interest group is the Daimyō which starts out with clout in the forties. It has inefficient backwards laws such as Peasant Levies, Serfdom, Local Police Force, Hereditary Bureaucrats, Closed Borders, Traditionalism and Land-Based Taxation. Such laws restrict a more efficiently run economy and serve to strengthen the Daimyō interest group. (As a saving grace, however, the country does has Slavery Banned.) Furthermore, it is insufficient in Government Administration buildings, resulting in low bureaucracy (though it is not in deficit) and tax inefficiency. It also has very few Construction Sectors, thus making the building of new industry and resources a slow endeavor. And lastly, it is Isolationist and does not trade any form of goods with any other country (except small quantities with the [Netherlands](Netherlands.md) and [Qing](China.md) by treaty) and will have to obtain all resources on its own or through [treaties](Treaty.md) from the beginning.

### Military

Japan's military is also in a weird in-between state where the army of 100 irregulars is obsolete against the European powers, not nearly strong enough to challenge Qing from the start, but is unnecessarily large to use against smaller Asian countries. Japan also has no navy, lacking even a single flotilla. This will hinder Japan from protecting its coasts and will prevent the use of its armies for naval invasions, making offensive wars as good as impossible before significant investments has been made in the navy. Therefore it can be a consideration to delete 70-80% of it to save costs. This has political ramifications though because all those former officers will likely switch to support the Daimyō instead.

The island of Hokkaido remains mostly uncolonized and the island of Sakhalin is ripe for potential [colonization](Colonization.md) after the country completes the Honorable Restoration, which grants Japan claims on Sakhalin. [Russia](Russia.md) also has a claim there, but since they have enough on their plate and no initial colonization institution, it usually takes a while before they get to even colonize the Island. Therefore there is no need to make haste colonizing at the expense of more pressing concerns.

## Goals

How Japan is played and what its goals are all depend on the player. Due to Japan having large resource potential and a sizable population, it is very possible to play tall with minimal expansion and to just build an industrial base in the island itself. However, you can also look outwards at the nations of [Korea](Korea.md), China, South East Asia and [Indonesia](Indonesia.md) to grow an empire (as Japan historically did in the later half of the game period).

The Honorable Restoration is optional, but it was the historic path Japan took and following it will significantly help you in industrializing and liberalizing (it is also needed to colonize Sakhalin). This guide assumes that you will be trying to finish the Honorable Restoration in a timely fashion.

## Tenpō Crisis

|  | Available only with the [Great Wave](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Great_Wave) DLC enabled. |
|---|---|

The Tenpō Crisis is an extremely difficult Journal Entry to accomplish as it historically failed. However, it is still possible to complete it.

The event has three buttons. By using Encourage Agricultural Industry in all your states, easily enable the "Combat Rice Hoarding" button to decrease the price of grain. Frugal Ordnance can be immediately enacted to apply an amendment to status order that reduces minimum [Standard of living](Standard of living.md), but we will change that law soon, so you have to wait if you want to keep the buff. Lastly, the Edo-Osaka Confiscation Order only requires two Naval Fortifications to be built, and grants a useful buff.

Next, are the goals. Here, we are striving for a balanced reward, as it is the easiest - and we're on a short time limit.

- Japan has not enacted Hereditary Bureaucrats
- Japan has 3 States with a level 3 Urban Center

- Japan has enacted Rigid Status Order
- Japan has enacted Shinsengumi

- No owned Japanese Homelands State is suffering from Famine
- Japan has a GDP of GDP of 14.5M or above
- Japanese army power projection is greater than 500
- We have 10 or more levels of Naval Fortification in owned States.

You already start with two goals - Army power projection, and No Famines. So you'll have to deal with the rest.

First, enact Rigid Status Order, as it'll boost the Landowner's approval. Then, enact Shinsengumi with the help of the Samurai. Lastly, enact Appointed Bureaucrats to get off Hereditary Bureaucrats.

Osaka and Kanto start with Urban Centers at about level 3 - you just need to build up one more state. Government Administration provides the most Urbanization for the least construction, so you can use them. Build up your GDP as per normal, and feel free to pass any modernizing laws as you go. Don't forget to build the 10 Naval fortifications!

## The Honorable Restoration

The Honorable Restoration requires one of two things: either the Daimyō must be weak enough where you can have a legitimate government without them, or you must defeat them in a revolution. At the start of the game both these options look like daunting tasks, as the Daimyō have over 40% clout, the support of the Shogun and a near monopoly on your government. However, over time, you can weaken them enough to make them just another interest group.

### Economy

Follow the beginner's guide to [early game industrialization](Beginner's guide.md) and set your taxes to the highest level – although this will cause radicals, you will want these radicals to help weaken the Daimyō. A strong peasant movement is especially useful. You should still be more careful with your budget than you normally are: Japan starts with a high [literacy](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Literacy) rate, which means you will have more radicals than you expect with high taxes, leading to more turmoil and tax waste; and interest is punishing for unrecognised countries. Immediately signing a [treaty](Treaty.md) to import some iron is a good idea, to run your construction sectors on iron-frame. China is a good bet, but any country with a surplus of iron will do.

The best states to make your industrial bases are Tohoku and Kanto – those states have a plethora of wood and iron, as well as a significant amount of peasants. Kyushu will have all the coal you need (later Hokkaido has a state trait for extra Coal Mines throughput) and there are a lot of sulfur and lead states in Japan as well. As a result of your very aristocratic government, you will have a lot of Authority, which means you can levy a lot of Road Maintenance decrees and consumption taxes.

Unlike some other countries, Japan has a lot of peasants. Even at 200+ construction sectors, you will have millions of peasants who you can give jobs. This means that you can continue on the early game construction loop for much longer than other countries, making Japan a very easy country to rapidly grow even without expansion, and you'll find yourself skyrocketing through the GDP leaderboard.

### Laws

[Bolstering](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Bolster) the Modernization Movement once it appears can help pass important laws, especially if their pressure causes the leader of an otherwise anti-reform interest group like the Daimyō to adopt their ideology.

With Tenant Farmers no longer reducing the power of the Daimyō compared to Serfdom, this law is less of a priority to pass. However, other laws remain important for modernizing:

- **Interventionism** or **Agrarianism**: Removes the debilitating Market Access Price Impact, investment pool efficiency and taxation capacity penalties of Traditionalism. Interventionism is the stronger law in the long run, but if it lacks support for whatever reason, Agrarianism will suffice.
- **Homesteading**: Removes +25% clout from the Daimyō. The Peasant Movement will actually dislike Tenant Farmers as much as Serfdom, so once their initial euphoria from enacting Tenant Farmers disappears, you may be able to use them to pass Homesteading. This will, however, make the Heimin much more powerful, which will impede post-Restoration law enactment for some industrial laws, so keep that in mind.
- **Dedicated Police Force**: Moving from Local Police Force to Dedicated Police Force will remove another +5% clout from the Daimyō. This is usually a relatively easy law to pass post-Tenant Farmers, and as a bonus will also make the Samurai happy.
- **Professional Army**: Another law that keeps the Samurai happy, and weakens the Daimyō. It does increase the cost of maintaining Japan's sizeable army though.
- **Elections:** Moving away from Autocracy and towards any form of election law (including Landed Voting, which may be the easiest one to pass) will reduce the Daimyō's clout as Autocracy increases the political strength of Aristocrats.

Several other measures to reduce the Daimyō's power as needed:

**Forced to open markets**: At some point in the run, [the United Kingdom](Great Britain.md), [the United States](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/USA), or [France](France.md) will send a diplomatic demand for investment rights. *Do not decline* this demand – it will be near impossible for you to win, especially if it's being sent by the UK or France (and they may add treaty ports as secondary demands during the play). Accepting this demand will immediately get you Free Trade and will give you an event that gives the Daimyō a −75% clout modifier for the duration of the Restoration. This makes it trivial to progress the Honorable Restoration.

You may also occasionally be able to force this by starting a diplomatic play against a British colony in Australia or North America. This will bring the United Kingdom into the war and they may set investment rights as a war goal against Japan. This is a risk however, as they might instead demand a treaty port or conquer a state. A strong navy will not be needed as the goal of this play is to back down during the Diplomatic Phase and allow the United Kingdom to impose their terms upon Japan, immediately opening your market.

**Firing Shogun generals**: Replacing starting generals with ones from other interest groups is a very easy way to affect clout.

**Fighting the revolution**: If there are any laws that the Daimyō and a movement dislike but no other interest group dislikes, then you might want to just force a revolution if it's beatable. This will immediately finish the Honorable Restoration without needing to wait for 10 years.

Note that implementing a [school system](Economy laws.md) is not as much of a concern as it is for many other unrecognized powers, since Japan already has a good [literacy](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Literacy) as well as the Journal Entry [Terakoya System](Japanese content.md) which provides [education access](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Education_access) both for peasants specifically and the population in general.

After passing Homesteading, or after Tenant Farmers if you don't want to go Homesteading, you will want to reduce taxes back to High to prevent too many radicals and turmoil. At this point in the run you may also have Atmospheric Engine, so it's a good idea to start industrializing with the iron/coal/tools/wood loop.

After completing some or all of these and reducing taxes back to High, you should have opportunities to reform government to get a non-Shogun government that has over 50 [legitimacy](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Legitimacy). If the government has between 45 and 50 legitimacy, lower taxes might help put it above the threshold. This will immediately trigger the Honorable Restoration journal entry, and will continue progressing it as long as you have over 50 legitimacy and is at peace. After 10 years (more precisely 120 months which need not be continuous), or after defeating the Daimyō in a revolution, you will have completed the Honorable Restoration. At this point in the game you will have a solid industrial base, a relatively modernized army (or at least the technology to do so), and a more stable political situation. Now, you can continue to grow your economy, look outwards to the rest of Asia, expand your colonial empire, and/or try to earn recognition from the Great Powers.

## Diplomacy and earning recognition

Due to a secure island position and a relatively strong army, it is actually difficult for [Russia](Russia.md) or [Qing](China.md) to invade you, especially once you discover Line Infantry. However, it is still better to keep cordial relations with them up until you complete the Honorable Restoration and have a solid industrial base. At that point, Russia becomes a juicy target to militarily earn recognition, and Qing may implode at any time leading to a lot of opportunities to expand.

The other great powers, such as the United Kingdom, the United States, and France, are worth keeping an eye on. If one of them becomes Belligerent towards you, that's not too much of a worry – all they want to do is open your market, which helps you in completing the Restoration. Once you've been opened up, however, it's a good idea to try to improve relations with one of the three (ideally the UK or France) so that you can get a defensive pact with them. If you're lucky, you may have lobbies that will help you with this.

Peacefully earning recognition is trivial due to Japan's large GDP as long as you manage to get rid of Traditionalism in good time, though it will likely take 5–10 years of waiting. To speed it up, you can defeat a Great Power or a Major Power in a war. The easiest way to do this is to abuse the Russian AI: they do not defend Siberia from naval invasions. This means that you can set a few war goals on the far eastern Siberian states (liberating Yakutia, Kamchatka, and war reparations will be enough), have a strong Line Infantry or Skirmish Infantry stack defending your homeland from naval invasions, and then send one cavalry unit to invade Siberia and take all the states you need, and then just wait until their war score drops to the negatives.

If you don't want to exploit the Russian AI, you will need to fight them conventionally. If Qing has imploded, it's a good idea to take the Manchurian states so that you have a front with the Russians in Siberia. Alternatively, you may need to fight a Major Power or another Great Power. Do note that enforcing war goals on or liberating/transferring the [Dutch East Indies](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Dutch_East_Indies) or the [Philippines](Philippines.md) will not count as war goals on the Netherlands or Spain for the purposes of earning recognition. Play diplomatically, be vigilant about looking at the geopolitical situation and finding allies, improve your military and navy, and you'll eventually find a good opportunity for a war that can earn you recognition.

## Colonization

Hokkaido and Sakhalin are the only two initial options for colonization, as you start with the Frontier Colonization law. Japan will not start with claims on Sakhalin whereas [Russia](Russia.md) will, making it uncolonizable until you complete the Honorable Restoration which gives you a claim. However, if you are proactive in trying to complete the Restoration you will generally finish it before Russia even thinks about colonizing Sakhalin. If you plan on being hostile towards Russia it's a good idea to colonize Sakhalin as fast as possible and to not let Russia get it – it is directly connected to Hokkaido, which means Russia will have a land front with you during any wars.

Once Hokkaido and/or Sakhalin are done and there are no further plans for colonization, the colonial law can be reset to No Colonial Affairs to save bureaucracy. Alternatively, you can look to other land to colonize, such as the Pacific Islands, Papua New Guinea and Africa. The Pacific Islands, Papua New Guinea and the accessible areas of Africa have the malaria trait and Quinine needs to be researched before these lands can be colonized. The colonization law also have to be changed away from Frontier Colonization.

## Expansion

Once Japan’s military has been built up, you may choose to expand further into [Korea](Korea.md), Manchuria, Formosa, Southern China, [Hawaii](Hawaii.md), Southeast Asia, the Middle East and/or southern Africa. Historically, Japan had a large colonial empire in Asia consisting of Manchuria, Korea, various Southeast Asian countries, and all of Indonesia and the Philippines.

Annexing any part of [Qing](China.md) will require that Japan's military be at least one technology ahead of Qing's. If you have researched Line Infantry, Napoleonic Warfare and Army Reserves before Qing and have an adequate navy, that should be enough to give you the edge against Qing. This doesn't mean you can easily defeat Qing, as Qing still has a massive manpower pool compared to Japan. The strategy is to defend whatever land border Japan has with Qing and then navally invade and occupy parts of Beijing before setting that army into the defensive. It is also easy to occupy all of Formosa, so that can be an easy target to snipe war reparations.

Manchuria and Formosa are good sources of wood and Manchuria provides a front for a Russian war in the future. Especially Manchuria is a priority, since it is required for one of Japan's [flavored companies, the South Manchuria Railway](List of flavored companies.md). Korea can be liberated to make them a friend later or for future conquest (completing the Restoration can give you claims, greatly reducing the infamy cost of abbreviation). You can also choose to annex the southern Qing states of Guangdong, Guangxi, Guizhou and Yunnan. These states will have less wood compared to Manchuria but Yunnan does have an abundance of opium. Pretty much all Chinese states have a high amount of population and peasants which is great for industrialization, although you may need to liberalize your citizenship laws to at least Racial Segregation to increase the acceptance of Han and other Chinese culture pops. Integrating these states only takes 5 years, but is not advisable as their huge population of illiterate peasants will drag down your national literacy score.

Southeast Asia is abundant in gold, rubber, and oil. Countries such as [Brunei](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Brunei), [Sulu](Sulu.md) and others on the island of Borneo can often be beaten by a naval invasion of only a single flotilla, giving you a land front to invade other countries. The same goes for [Hawaii](Hawaii.md) in the Pacific.

In the Middle East, you can choose to vassalize or annex [Oman](Oman.md) (or [Zanzibar](Zanzibar.md) if it exists) and use its east African territory as a springboard to colonize eastern Africa. [Bahrain](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Bahrain) is also often a very easy target for invading as they tend to have no or very few battalions.

In Africa, the Senegalese countries of [Futa Jallon](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Futa_Jallon), [Kaarta](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Kaarta), [Segou](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Segou) and [Massina](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Massina) are options for conquest. And in South Africa, there are [Zulu](Zulu.md), [Oranje](Oranje.md) and [Transvaal](Transvaal.md) who all have a lot of gold mines and rarely any diplomatic backers.
