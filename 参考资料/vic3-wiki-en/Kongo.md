**Kongo** is an unrecognized power in Equatorial Africa. With limited starting technology and economic potential, it is highly difficult for Kongo to become an influential power in its region or the wider world.

## Historical background

Kongo is a broken nation in 1836, a shadow of its former glory as a powerful Central African kingdom. At its height in the late 15th and early 16th centuries, Kongo controlled a vast territory and traded extensively with European powers, including [Portugal](Portugal.md). However, by the 17th century, Kongo had begun to decline due to internal conflict, the slave trade, and the arrival of European colonizers. By the 19th century, Kongo was a fractured nation, with numerous small polities vying for power and control over the region.

Despite this, Kongo retained a rich cultural heritage, with a highly developed artistic tradition and a sophisticated court culture. The kingdom was also known for its production of high-quality cloth and its skilled metalworking. In the face of European colonialism, Kongo struggled to maintain its independence and sovereignty, but its legacy as a once-great African kingdom continued to inspire later generations.

## Politics

At the start of the game, Kongo is a Monarchy with Manikongo André Kinlaza, a Traditionalist, supporting the Landowners. Kongo starts with Traditionalism which weakens the [Investment pool](Treasury.md), making economic growth very difficult. Additionally, Kongo starts with no [institutions](Institutions.md).

### Interest groups

The Landowners begin in a strongly dominant position, with almost no opposition and many laws also strongly favoring them.

- The Landowners start in government.
- The Armed Forces, Catholic Church, and Rural Folk are in opposition.
- The Trade Unions, Petite Bourgeoisie, Intelligentsia, and Industrialists are marginalized.

### Diplomacy

Kongo starts with no diplomatic relations and its only active interest is in the Congo region itself.

[Portugal](Portugal.md) and [Egypt](Egypt.md) start with engaged interests in the region, and other European powers are likely to gain interests in Congo as well as they begin to colonize Africa.

### Industry and Resources

**Industry**

Kongo begins with no industry, and only a single level of Millet Farm, with the rest of its economy in subsistence farming.

**Resources**

Kongo has access to iron and eventually rubber. Additionally, Kongo has access to most agricultural resources, except for silk and opium.

## Military

Kongo has the Peasant Levies law.

Kongo starts with five Battalions and can call upon nine Conscripts. All Barracks use No Organized Training. The battalions compose a single army.

| Army name | Battalions | General | HQ region |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st Kongolese Army | 5 Irregular Infantry | — | Equatorial Africa |

Kongo has no flotillas.

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Kongo
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.
Kongo begins in a tough place; surrounded by decentralized nations and [Portuguese colonies](Portugal.md), it has no military expansion possibilities. Colonization requires researching at least Colonization, but even then most of its surrounding states cannot be colonized before researching Quinine due to the Severe Malaria present in them. The states of Congo, Lower Congo, North Angola and Gabon are homelands of Kongo's primary culture, Bakongo, but outside of those, colonization is limited.

Economically, becoming a protectorate of a European power, such as Portugal or Spain, is likely the quickest route to having a large enough market for growth. Focusing on cash crops such as tea and dye is easiest. Additionally, the Congolian Rainforest gives a bonus to hardwood, so logging is another option.
