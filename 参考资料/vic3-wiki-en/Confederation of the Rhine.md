**Confederation of the Rhine** is a [formable country](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Formable_country) for most countries with North German or South German culture.

If Confederation of the Rhine exists, no country can form [Germany](Germany.md). Thus, Confederation of the Rhine cannot form Germany, but it can form [Central Europe](Central Europe.md).[1]

## Historical context

The Confederation of the Rhine was historically established during the Napoleonic Wars. At its greatest extent, it included all of the German states except for Austria and Prussia and the parts of Germany controlled by Denmark and Sweden. It was dissolved in 1813 after the French were defeated in Germany.

The historical Confederation of the Rhine was not a unified state, but a loose confederation of the various German states as clients of the French Empire. It used the French flag while its constituent states retained their own flags.

## Formation

11 of the 14 core states are needed in order to form Confederation of the Rhine
The Confederation of the Rhine can be formed by any of the following countries once they have researched Nationalism and own at least 11 of the 14 core states. [Austria](Austria.md), [Prussia](Prussia.md), [North German Federation](North German Federation.md), and [South German Federation](South German Federation.md) are specifically blocked from forming the Confederation of the Rhine.

### Journal formation

If a Great Power has at least 1 non-Personal Union subjects in the Rhine, North Germany, or South Germany regions covering at least 4 states, it may turn them into the Confederation of the Rhine through a journal entry. This action does **not** require to control subjects across 11 states nor to have Nationalism researched; even two subjects controlling the same split state are enough. Other subjects that have North German or South German as a primary culture, as well as unincorporated and unclaimed states with homelands of those cultures, may be added to the Confederation. Note that this method does not change the Confederation's primary culture or religion. It is even possible for the Confederation to not have any German primary culture.

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Confederation of the Rhine
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

### As a Great Power subject

As a Great Power, it can be useful to have the Confederation as a subject. This particularly applies to [France](France.md) and [Scandinavia](Scandinavia.md), who have a vested interest in blocking the formation of [Germany](Germany.md). Some North German minors will leave the British customs union during the Hanoverian succession; they are potentially up for grabs to form the Confederation. Similarly, [Schleswig](Schleswig.md) and [Holstein](Holstein.md) are subjects under [Denmark](Denmark.md); note that the Schlewsig-Holstein question is considered resolved if they are no longer Denmark's subjects. As subjects of subjects are considered for the journal formation, making Denmark a protectorate can also aid in forming the Confederation; if the Confederation is formed this way, it may become the junior member of a personal union with Denmark.

Note that if [Switzerland](Switzerland.md) is a subject of the overlord when the Confederation is formed, it will also be annexed by the Confederation.

### Expanding the Confederation

The Confederation's overlord can expand the Confederation every three years by allowing it to annex any unincorporated German territory held by the overlord or the overlord's German subjects in three regions: Rhine, North Germany and South Germany. Consider beating up Prussia and forcing it to release e.g. [Pomerania](Pomerania.md), and then acquire the released German nations as subjects to be later annexed by the Confederation. Note that the expansion function will be unavailable once there are no Great Powers among the North German and South German nations. [Danzig](Danzig.md) and the Baltic Governorates ( [United Baltic Provinces](United Baltic Provinces.md)) will not be annexed by the Confederation as their capitals are typically outside the relevant regions.
