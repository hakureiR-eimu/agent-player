The **Hudson’s Bay Company** (called *Rupert's Land* when independent) is a minor power in northern America. It is a colonial chartered company of [Great Britain](Great Britain.md), owned by the [Hudson’s Bay Company](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson%E2%80%99s_Bay_Company) and a good candidate to form [Canada](Canada.md).

## Scripted content

All Canadian countries have access to the following journal entry and decision. Note that none of these actually form Canada, which must be formed manually through the usual mechanics.

### Interest groups

Due to Hudson’s Bay Company's complete lack of historical IG leaders, the starting interest groups in
government can vary.
Hudson’s Bay Company typically starts with the Industrialists in government. The Petite Bourgeoisie, Armed Forces, Rural Folk, Landowners and Trade Unions are in opposition, while the Intelligentsia and are marginalized.

The Industrialists have the Colonialist ideology instead of the default Laissez-Faire.

### Diplomacy

Hudson’s Bay Company is a chartered company of [Great Britain](Great Britain.md), owned by the Hudson’s Bay Company, and thus a member of the *British Empire* power bloc. It is bordered by fellow British colonies [Columbia District](Oregon.md) to the west, [Upper Canada](Ontario.md) and [Lower Canada](Quebec.md) to the southeast, and their overlord [Great Britain](Great Britain.md) in Labrador. It also borders the independent [United States](America.md) to the south, as well as a few native decentralized nations.

The Hudson's Bay Company has claims on and active colonies in the [Iron Confederacy](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Iron_Confederacy) and [Niitsitapi](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Niitsitapi).

### Industry and resources

**Industry**

Despite its huge size, the Hudson's Bay Company begins with no industrial or agricultural buildings.

**Resources**

The Hudson's Bay Company starts with only 5 Logging Camps, but its lands are rich in many resources, including all necessary for early industrialization. Being part of the [British](Great Britain.md) market will likely account for any needs Hudson's Bay Company - and Canada - may have in other regards.

## Strategy and guides

| ![Ambox outdated info.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/6/65/Ambox_outdated_info.png/22px-Ambox_outdated_info.png) | Please help with verifying or updating this guide. It was last verified for version 1.9. |
|---|---|

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Hudson’s Bay Company
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.
**Starting Situation**

The Hudson’s Bay Company starts out controlling the lion's share of the Canadian lands, but has a relatively sparse population. Being a British colony, it starts out with access to the British Market, arguably the best market in the game at game start.

**Grand Strategy**

The most obvious path forward is to form Canada. The 'Confederate Canada' journal entry railroads this process. After Canada is united, the path forward is less obvious. Canada usually doesn't have the population or economic base to fight [America](America.md). Going for independence may be appealing, but then the player is vulnerable to America. It is inadvisable to significantly invest in the territories of Oregon, Washington, and Idaho as America has scripted events to take custody of them. Unless America collapses due to Civil War, the player will almost certainly lose those territories. America also has scripted events to take custody of Alaska from Russia, but it often doesn't.

The Hudson’s Bay Company starts out with uncolonized lands within its borders. Colonizing these lands may cause small conflicts, but will give access to more population and resources. It will also allow full integration of its lands for extra infrastructure and tax revenue.

A decent economic strategy is to focus on natural resource extraction, and hope the British Market provides the rest of the industrial goods you need. This will not work for forever (the [East India Company](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/East_India_Company) is also in the British market and its population makes it a bottomless pit for manufactured goods) but it will be ok at the start.

If Britain starts to fall into internal disfunction (as it often does) independence may be the path forward, as it takes the player out of the British market. Still, newly independent status often yields significant market disruptions and diplomatic vulnerability. Note that as a Chartered Company, the HBC cannot be independent until it has researched *Civilizing Mission*.

The main internal issue the Hudson’s Bay Company will have in the mid to late game is population management. It will struggle to staff its industries and armies, and this struggle will be a recurring theme throughout the game. There are many ways to address this: liberalizing immigration policies, conquering underdeveloped but highly populated regions abroad, and so forth.

The player may want to take Greenland and Iceland off of [Denmark](Denmark.md) to better control their area. The threat usually isn't Denmark itself, rather, it is Denmark being conquered or subjugated by a more aggressive power, and them using those areas as a springboard into the Canadian lands. [Russia](Russia.md) is a threat, but usually neglects its Alaskan province to fight in Central Asia and Eastern Europe. In fact, the player can usually conquer Alaska relatively easily since the Russian AI will not defend it. Other than that, the only direct geographic threat is America, who is usually easy to maintain good relations with.

**Internal Politics**

The Hudson’s Bay Company's starting policies aren't bad, relatively speaking, and the player shouldn't have too much trouble getting the [Laws](Laws.md) they want. [No Migration Controls](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/No_Migration_Controls) is a great policy for the Hudson’s Bay Company. [Total Separation](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Total_Separation) and [Multiculturalism](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Multiculturalism) will help the player bring in as many immigrants as possible.

**Technologies**

Note that Joint-Stock Companies is **not** useful at all, since as a company HBC cannot establish companies on their own.

**Diplomacy**

A Hudson’s Bay Company will have two main diplomatic choices:

- What to do after Canada is united
- Whether or not to stay under Britain

A Hudson’s Bay Company player already has the technology and policy to colonize in places other than Canada, but may not have the military or population to do so. Still, a player that does reach out abroad will primarily do so to bring immigrants back to Canada.

Staying under Britain is also an important call to make. Canada's low population will make independence difficult, but a player may run into issues if they keep losing money to overlord income transfer and losing market access to the British Market from convoy raids. The British start with the best navy in the game, and usually has one of the best navies throughout the game. Independence may be difficult without external help or Britain falling into chaos.

**Potential**

The Hudson’s Bay Company starts out with access to a treasure trove of natural resources, access to the sea, and in a good market. It only needs to maintain good relations with a few nations, and doing so isn't hard. All of the pieces required to build a strong economy are there. If the player maintains good relations with America and Britain, they will have little in the way of threats to worry about. This will allow the player to pick and choose their fights throughout the game.

**Weaknesses**

Population, population, and population again. Population is a soft cap to economic growth and military expansion, and the Hudson’s Bay Company will be soft capped early and often. If America or Britain attempt to conquer the Canadian lands directly, the Hudson’s Bay Company (and later Canada) probably will not have the population to resist for some time, if ever.
