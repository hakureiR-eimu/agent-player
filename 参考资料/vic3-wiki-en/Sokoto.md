*"Under the banner of the Fulani Jihad, the Sokoto Caliphate is expanding rapidly. Can Sokoto unite West Africa and resist the Western powers?"*

**Sokoto** is an unrecognized regional power in West Africa. While geographically large and more powerful than its immediate neighbors, Sokoto is underdeveloped and liable to be conquered or subjugated by a European power.

### Interest groups

Sokoto starts with the Landowners and Sunni Ulema in government. The Armed Forces and Intelligentsia are in opposition, while the Rural Folk, Petite Bourgeoisie, Trade Unions, and Industrialists are marginalized.

### Industry and resources

**Industry**

Sokoto has only one Textile Mill and a moderate number of agricultural buildings.

**Resources**

Sokoto has access to a good amount of coal, as well as some iron and lead. It also has a large amount of arable land and can grow most cash crops.

## Strategy and guides

| ![Ambox outdated info.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/6/65/Ambox_outdated_info.png/22px-Ambox_outdated_info.png) | Please help with verifying or updating this guide. It was last verified for version 1.10. |
|---|---|

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Sokoto
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.
**Starting Situation**

Sokoto is sizable, has a decent population, and has decent resource availability. However, it is technologically poor and economically underdeveloped.

**Grand Strategy**

Sokoto's primary concern is to play economic and technological-catch up in order to minimize European influence in West Africa. Unlike some other parts of the world, the Europeans already have small bits of land in West Africa at the start of the game, and the Europeans are more held back by Malaria than the status of their army. The time window for the Europeans to acquire Quinine is a tremendous opportunity for Sokoto.

At this point, the player can go for 'Wide' Sokoto or 'Tall' Sokoto.

- "Wide" will expand their army, early on, to grab up as many West and Central African minor powers as possible. This will deny the Europeans land and population. This could be either direct conquest or [vassalizing](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Vassal). Direct conquest is almost always more useful, but vassalizing generates significantly less [Infamy](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Infamy). The hope is that the Europeans will be less likely to attack as single large power rather than numerous small, weak ones.
- "Tall" Sokoto will not expand, and instead focus on internal development. Obviously, good ports and bare necessity resources are vital, but other than that, little land conquest will be performed. 'Tall' Sokoto will build [Construction Sectors](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Construction_Sector) where 'Wide' Sokoto will build armies. This will give 'Tall' Sokoto more power to sort out internal issues with economy and technology. On the flip side, this will leave the other African minors for the Europeans, and it will be more to take off the Europeans later.

The Europeans – notably [France](France.md) or [Great Britain](Great Britain.md) – will almost certainly attempt to conquer or colonize chunks of West Africa. Occasionally, [America](America.md), [Netherlands](Netherlands.md) or [Spain](Spain.md) may show up as well.

Once the player hits a wall with conquest, the next decision is their overall diplomatic approach: Protectorate or Independent. Becoming a Protectorate under a European power will have a lot of market and security benefits, but will limit diplomatic options and may be difficult to get rid of later. Staying independent will be difficult with an underdeveloped Army, but not impossible with some good diplomacy. Even if the player chooses to remain independent, joining a [power bloc](Power bloc.md) might work well for them.

A player may consider growing through colonization, and there is a lot of colonization to be done in Africa at game start. However, a lot of that colonization is effectively gated by technology (via [Malaria](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Malaria) and [Severe Malaria](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Severe_Malaria)), which is one of Sokoto's weaknesses. Sokoto can colonize, but it will be beaten to the punch by the European majors.

**Internal Politics**

Sokoto's starting laws aren't very good, but it doesn't have the tech to replace many of them.

**Diplomacy**

Sokoto's primary concern will probably be keeping the European powers out it is lands. However, it can latch on to inter-European wars to conquer the European West African colonies if the Europeans don't do a good job defending said colonies, which they often don't. The colonial powers' armies will be busy in Europe, leaving lightly defended colonies that can be swarmed with sheer numbers. Other than that, it should avoid any conflicts with the Europeans without direct support.

**Potential**

If Sokoto can salvage its initial situation and gain control of West Africa, it can be a global power. West Africa gains significant wealth from Rubber and Oil later in the game, and has a lot of population and resources. Sokoto is ultimately about outlasting the early-game technology issues.

**Weaknesses**

The European West African ports can serve as a bridgehead to invade Sokoto's acquired lands, making Sokoto have a *de facto* direct border with some of the most powerful countries in the game for some time. With some clever diplomacy and good fortune, Sokoto may be able to capture those ports, but not in a 1-on-1 fight.
