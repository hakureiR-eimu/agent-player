While wars are mostly won by land troops, naval power cannot be discounted. Any country which has overseas territory, overseas subjects, or overseas trade is susceptible to convoy raiding, which can cripple industry as overseas market access is severely reduced so that goods cannot travel from one region to another. Additionally, fighting overseas enemies requires transporting armies and maintaining supply lines, both of which can also be raided, and may also require naval invasions to establish a land front. Finally, the mere presence of a powerful navy boosts a nations amongst the world's powers.

## Ships

Naval units are called *Ships*. Ships are constructed using Ship Construction, which is produced by Shipyards. Ships are always organized into one or more formations called *Fleets*. Each Ship requires sufficient sailors in order to operate. Sailors are generated by *naval administrations*, which can be built in any state, not just coastal states. Each fully employed naval administration produces 1000 sailors. Fleets can move through [straits](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Strait) only when the strait controllers permit the fleet's owner transit.

### Supply ships

Supply ships are a type of ship that are used to supply military ships already on the water and overseas armies. A certain number of supply ships are needed in order to resupply a navy, and ships will sink with insufficient supply. Supply ship construction allocation determines how much ship construction is allocated to the production of supply ships.

### Ship types

Naval units are divided into four categories: *Capital Ships*, *Cruisers*, *Torpedo Craft*, and *Supply Ships*. Fleet movement speed is determined by the slowest ship in the fleet. Other stats are individual to each ship, though the fleet viewer shows an averaged value of each stat across the fleet.

Within the four categories, ships are further subdivided into different hull types, typically divided by technological advances:

- Capital Ships:
  - Ship of the Line

### Veterancy

Ships gain veterancy while on missions and during battles. Each level of veterancy provides the following bonuses:

| Veterancy level | Effects | Total exp. |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | +5 Accuracy +5% Critical chance | 100 |
| Seasoned | +10 Accuracy +10% Critical chance | 200 |
| Veteran | +15 Accuracy +10% Critical chance +10% Readiness gain | 400 |
| Elite | +25 Accuracy +15% Critical chance +20% Readiness gain | 800 |

## Missions

Fleets are assigned to one or more sea nodes with a specified mission, the more nodes a fleet is assigned to, the less effective it is in each. A fleet's mission can be changed from the fleet screen.

| Mission | Description | Notable modifier |
|---|---|---|
| Project Power | Fleet generates interest in adjacent strategic regions | — |
| Interception | Fleet attempts to intercept enemy fleets | — |
| Protect Supply Lines | Fleet attempts to protect supply lines | — |
| Raid Supply Lines | Fleet attempts to raid enemy supply lines | **−25%** Ship visibility |
| Blockade | Fleet blockades enemy coastal states | — |
| Port Bombardment | Fleet bombards enemy states, causing devastation and damaging coastal buildings | — |
| Privateering | Fleet attempts to capture enemy trade | **−25%** Ship visibility (recognized countries; targets a specific hostile's imports/exports) |
| The missions below require Sphere of Influence to be enabled |  |  |
| Hunt Pirates | Fleet hunts pirates and privateers | — |
| Piracy | Fleet attempts to capture foreign trade | **+50%** Piracy goods capacity, **+25%** Ship vulnerability, **−50%** Ship visibility (unrecognized countries only) |

## Sea nodes

By clicking on the Bay of Biscay sea node (the triangle on the node indicates that it has been selected) and then by clicking on the navy tab, it is possible to see which fleets are doing what in the node. Note that fleets on convoy escort also show up in the “patrolling the coast” box, and these fleets do not get involved in a battle between friendly raiding fleets and enemy patrolling fleets – so if a battle starts between the 35-strong British raiding fleet and the 60-strong French patrolling fleet, the 100-strong British fleet on convoy escort will not get involved.
Fleets operate in sea nodes (the blue or golden circles connected by dashed lines on the ocean – which represent sea and ocean areas). Most sea nodes are connected to a number of state regions, which can be seen by zooming in and following the smaller dashed lines that appear from the node to each region's port province.

Fleets' missions apply only when a fleet is deployed to a sea zone.

Sea nodes also partly determine the merchant marine cost of trade routes and supply ship cost of supply lines, with the cost increasing as the route goes through more nodes.

### Presence

While stationed in a sea node, fleets build up *presence* at a rate of 2% per day. The effectiveness of a fleet's orders are scaled by its presence in the sea node.

## Naval invasion

naval invasion requires both a fleet and one or two armies. The armies can either be an army of marines embarked on the fleet, a regular army transported for the occasion, or both. Naval invasions cannot be initiated if armies has less power projection than the naval fortification invasion defense of the state.

Once the invasion is confirmed, if a regular army has been selected, the fleet first regroups with it on a friendly coastal state and then travels to the sea node adjacent to the targeted state. For the invasion to proceed, the sea node must be uncontested to establish a beachhead. The sea node is *contested* if any defending fleet of at least 25% of the attacker fleet's power projection is deployed there. Once the army has landed, contesting the sea has no effect.

It takes two weeks for battles to begin. Defender's armies stationed in the region HQ **do participate** in the defense of a naval invasion. However, in case of multiple invasions, it is better to assign armies specifically to each one, as a stationed army could commit all its battalions to a single battle and leave the other invasion uncovered.

There is a *Naval Invasion Efficiency* penalty of **−25%** to attack for every invading battalions that is not of Marines type.

The naval invasion fails if the invading fleet loses any naval battle or if the army loses three battles while having 0% occupation of the targeted state's territory. Then the army and fleet travel back to their home HQ.

Once the targeted state is fully occupied, the invasion is successful and a new regular front is created.

### Naval Fortification

[Naval Fortifications](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Naval_Fortification) fortify a coastal state against naval assaults and naval invasions. They provide resistance against naval invasions, blockades, and bombardments.

## Naval battles

A French patrolling fleet has caught a British fleet assigned to raid convoys in the Bay of Biscay. Note the low starting morale of the British fleet (it was still recovering from an earlier battle).
If opposing fleets with opposing orders are in the same zone for long enough, a naval battle occurs. An anchor in a circle is displayed over the sea node on-screen, and both fleets engage each other. There is no longer a single unified offense/ defense stat pair for ships — combat now resolves through the fuller stat set in the tables above (accuracy vs. vulnerability determines hits, hull/crew damage vs. armor determines damage dealt, critical hit chance/multiplier adds burst damage, screening affects escort/interception), still split into an attacking side (naval invasion / convoy-raid interceptors) and a defending side (convoy raiders / anti-invasion fleets). Similar to land battles, there are also battle conditions for both sides that can have a significant influence on the outcome.

As is the case for land battles, naval battles continue (unless the war ends first) until one side loses all morale.

### Share of flotillas

The current formula:

- Starts from each side's ships not currently in another battle (not simply "available flotillas in the fleet").
- Is capped by the commanding admiral's command limit (half of it, floored at the no-commander default if that would be lower); fleets without a commander are capped at the flat no-commander default (/Victoria 3/game/MILITARY_FORMATION_COMMAND_LIMIT_NO_COMMANDER = 20).
- Is then adjusted by a factor comparing the side's per-ship *detection* against the enemy's per-ship *visibility* — a side that detects better relative to how visible the enemy fleet is brings comparatively more ships to the fight. This ties directly into the detection/visibility stats in the ship tables above (Seaplane Tenders and Aircraft Carriers, at 150/300 Detection, are far ahead of every other ship type here).

### Naval battle conditions

Naval battle conditions represent the weather during a naval battle. They affect both sides of the battle and are strongly influenced by the sea region's current [harvest condition](Harvest condition.md), if any. Several conditions deal periodic damage to hull or crew in addition to modifying combat stats, with amount scaled by the intensity of the condition.

| Naval condition | Weight, modified by weather | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Calm Waters | 1 ×10 if Calm Waters | No combat modifier |
| Cyclone | 0 +15 if Cyclone | −30% Accuracy −30% Speed −30% Readiness gain −30% Screening Every eight battle turns: 30 Scaled hull damage 10 Scaled Crew damage |
| Fog | 1 ×10 if Fog | −40% Accuracy −10% Speed |
| Ice | 0 +10 if Ice | −20% Speed Every 16 battle turns: 30 Scaled hull damage 10 Scaled Crew damage |
| Rough Waters | 1 ×10 if Rough Waters | −10% Accuracy −20% Speed Every six battle turns: 5 Scaled hull damage 1 Scaled Crew damage |
| Strong Winds | 1 ×10 if Strong Winds | −20% Accuracy −10% Screening |
| Tropical Storm | 0 +10 if Tropical Storm +5 if Cyclone | −10% Speed −20% Readiness gain −10% Screening Every 20 battle turns: 3 Scaled hull damage 2 Scaled Crew damage |

### Morale recovery

After a battle has ended, flotillas gradually recover morale over time (and ships, if sunk, are rebuilt). It is possible for an admiral's forces to begin another battle without having recovered their morale entirely.

## Supply

Formations fighting overseas rely on the [supply network](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Supply_network) to keep them equipped and in supply. If enemy fleets raid the supply network, sinking convoys, this impacts on the supply of formations, which has a direct impact on the morale recovery of units. For example, if raiding has reduced a supply network's efficiency by 2%, then the morale recovery of units overseas is 98%. If supply is reduced beyond 95%, it applies an equal penalty to organization. For example, a unit with 80% supply would have a 20% penalty to maximum organization.

Additionally, [shortages](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Shortage) for units' buildings reduce a formations supply to the same result.

### Attrition

All mobilized formations are subject to attrition, which leads to both wounded and killed pops. The impact of this can be substantial and can outstrip the number of wounded and killed from battle. Formations receive **−50%** less attrition while stationed at their home HQ.

Base attrition is **10%** and casualties from attrition can range from **4%** to **12%** weekly.

Battalions actively engaged in a battle temporarily do not suffer attrition.
