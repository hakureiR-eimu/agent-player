**Agitators** are characters outside the formal political scene and administration, who support [political movements](Political movement.md) aligned with their [ideology](Character ideology.md) or [interest group](Interest group.md), increasing those movements' pop attraction.

## Agitator mechanics

| Source | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base | **+1** |
| Major power or higher rank | **+1** |
| Labor Movement | **+1** |
| Political Agitation | **+1** |
| Mass Propaganda | **+1** |

Each country can support only a certain number of agitators at one time. All countries have a base of 1 *agitator slot*, which is increased by 1 with each of the technologies: Labor Movement, Political Agitation, and Mass Propaganda. Great powers, major powers, and unrecognized major powers have an additional slot.

While a country has an empty agitator slot, it can either invite an exiled agitator or a new agitator can spawn randomly. Certain events and journal entries can also spawn agitators. The chance for a new agitator to spawn depends on a country's national literacy, with higher literacy increasing the chance.[1] For example, a country with 50% national literacy can expect a new agitator to spawn within about 7 years. Having more open slots does not increase the chance of a new agitator spawning.

When an agitator appears in or is invited to a country, they join a [political movement](Political movement.md) which aligns with their [ideology](Character ideology.md) or [interest group](Interest group.md). Agitators add 15 plus 0.15 times their popularity, whether positive or negative, to a movement's pop attraction. If an agitator's political movement disbands, the agitator joins a new political movement. If an agitator cannot support a political movement in their current country, they are exiled or retire.

Randomly generated agitators generally have ideologies that are opposed to the laws of the country. They are also more often affiliated with more populist interest groups, namely the Petite Bourgeoisie, Rural Folk, and Trade Unions. Agitators join political movements based on the scripted factors of each movement. This generally means they'll join the movement they are most ideologically aligned with, but occasionally they'll join other movements.
