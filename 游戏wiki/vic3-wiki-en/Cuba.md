*"Cuban relations with their Spanish overlords have become strained. Calls for representation in the Spanish parliament and resistance to the abolition of slavery have met with hostility from Madrid. Is a Cuban revolution inevitable?"*

**Cuba** is a minor power in the Caribbean region. Cuba was founded as a colony by [Spain](Spain.md) in the 16th century and is a colony of Spain at the start of the game.

Cuba is the primary contender to form the [Antillean Confederation](Antillean Confederation.md).

## Scripted content

With the Iberian Twilight DLC, Cuba access to a journal entry dealing with the independence movement, allowing it to potentially gain Afro-Caribeño as a primary culture.

### Interest groups

Cuba starts with Landowners and Armed Forces in government. The Catholic Church, Petite Bourgeoisie and Intelligentsia are in opposition.

Industrialists, Rural Folk, and Trade Unions are marginalized.

## Strategy and guides

| ![Ambox outdated info.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/6/65/Ambox_outdated_info.png/22px-Ambox_outdated_info.png) | Please help with verifying or updating this guide. It was last verified for version 1.12. |

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Cuba
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

### Opening

Cuba is in the unusual situation of starting with almost no infrastructure to spare. While Cuba also starts with Railways researched and can immediately build railways for infrastructure, at game start it may be prudent to consider building cheaper ports, which provide some infrastructure and will be needed for their Merchant Marine and Convoys soon as well.

At game start, Cuba's position in the Spanish market is somewhat advantageous, as it gives greater access to various important goods that Cuba is not producing yet, as well as demand for goods that can allow Cuba to industrialize comparatively quickly. Long term, however, the subject payments will be siphoning off a significant amount of Cuba's money, and iron shortages caused by Spain's attempts to expand its industry will make independence much more economically attractive once Cuba has established a decent base of production buildings to support its economy. Spain is also a relatively weak imperial power, and faces journal entries that further jeopardize its stability and military strength. This weakness can make it easier to secure independence, but it is also a liability prior to independence, as Cuba's fortunes will be somewhat tied to the extent that the more powerful countries like Great Britain and France try to prey on Spain, and Cuba's puppet status makes it impossible to seek additional allies except to declare independence.

Between Cuba's reliance on migration (rather than vast reserves of peasants) and its starting with an Education institution, it is possible to neglect University construction and still maintain sufficient Qualifications access to support an advanced economy. While this can be a useful shortcut early on, underdevelopment of universities will cause Cuba to fall behind in military technology and jeopardize its ability to defend itself against Great Powers.

### Seeking independence

Depending on circumstances, Cuba may or may not have to do much fighting against Spain to win its independence, as other Great Powers may be willing to support Cuba's independence or offer it in exchange for joining another war. However, it is nevertheless advisable to significantly increase the size of the Cuban military prior to independence in order to ward off other Great Powers looking to make Cuba their own. Naval strength is as, or more, important than the army, as decisively winning the naval battle guarantees victory against Spain.

With the Iberian Twilight DLC, Cuba also has the *Cuba es España* and *Independencia* Journal Entries, the former of which weakens the Landowners and increases liberty desire upon failure (completing the mission leads to the option for Spain to annex Cuba). *Independencia* offers a few distinct options to aid in decoupling Cuba from Spain: boosting the Intelligentsia to 10% influence will allow for Promise Freedom to Slaves, which will make the Slave Revolt movement increase liberty desire, and add both the abolition of slavery and Afro-Caribeño primary culture status once independence is accomplished. The elevation of Afro-Caribeño culture status is very powerful, as this means that between its two primary cultures, European and African heritage pops will have shared heritage trait bonuses to acceptance and can make Cuba a magnet for African immigrants in addition to competing for European ones, even with relatively restrictive citizenship laws (it will also make it more viable to annex states in the British Caribbean and Dixie with their Afro-American/Afro-Caribbean pops, as well as creating the prospect of unusually accepting colonies in Africa if Cuba pursues colonization there). The Prepare for War button will give a boost to Cuba's military power and liberty desire, and should be pressed as soon as the player is ready to commit to independence. Improving relations with the USA, alternatively, will create an opportunity for the player to switch to play as the USA, with a Cuban puppet.

Once independent, Cuba has sufficient iron, coal and sulfur to get an industrial economy off the ground, but will soon run out of resources to fuel expanded steel production. Luckily, most of the countries of Central and South America are eager for investment, and will accept foreign investment offers for little to nothing. From there, they can be invited into a power bloc (either a trade league, or any other type of bloc with the Market Unification principle at level 2 or better), and provide wood and ore for the whole market. Alternatively (or in addition), independent Cuba will likely be strong enough to defeat and quickly annex or make protectorates of countries in Central America and Haiti/Santo Domingo. More difficult to achieve, but still within reach, is trying to steal Puerto Rico, Jamaica, the Bahamas, and the West Indies from their European overlords. Late game, a Cuba with a developed military could take on the USA directly, hegemonize the Americas, or lead a global crusade against slavery.

### Achievements

Cuba is already a major producer of sugar from the outset of the game, so in practice getting *La vida dulce* and the [Sugar Rush](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Achievement) achievement is mostly a question of generally industrializing the economy and building enough trade centers to export more than 2000 units of goods to the world market. If need be, the sugar industry can be subsidized and expanded with the power of a well-developed mid to late game construction sector.

Cuba is in a good position for the *Can't Touch This* achievement, since it has more than 10% of GDP owned by [Spain](Spain.md) at the beginning of the game.
