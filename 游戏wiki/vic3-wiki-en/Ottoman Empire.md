The **Ottoman Empire** is a great power in the Middle East and southern Europe. It begins in a strong, but precarious position, with its growing reputation as the *Sick Man of Europe* hanging over its head.

The country's name changes to *Turkey* if it stops being a monarchy.

The glory days of the Ottoman Empire are long past. Once a global power, the Sublime Ottoman State has stagnated; corruption and dysfunction within the janissaries and bureaucracy has gained it the title – "the Sick Man of Europe". The empire's decline is evident in the recent independence of [Greece](Greece.md) and [Egypt](Egypt.md). To restore the glory of the Osman dynasty and the power of the Sublime Porte, many reforms must be accomplished.

The Ottoman Empire is one of four recommended countries for the *Economic Dominance* [objective](Objective.md), along with the [USA](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/USA), [Great Britain](Great Britain.md), and [Brazil](Brazil.md).

## Historical background

After being officially founded at the end of the 13th century (around the year 1299) by Osman I, the Ottoman Empire consolidated control of Anatolia and Greece and finally conquered the city of Constantinople on 29 May 1453, ending [Byzantium](Byzantium.md), the last remnant of the Roman Empire. In the following centuries, the power of the Ottoman Empire grew significantly. At its height, the empire controlled most of the Balkans, North Africa, and Mesopotamia, and the Ottoman emperors held the title of Caliph, the leader of the Muslim world. The Ottoman Empire's power began to wane in the 17th and 18th centuries, as its government institutions became more and more dysfunctional and its technological innovation stagnated.

Following defeats by [Russia](Russia.md) and [Austria](Austria.md) in the 1700s, the Greek Revolution in 1821-1829, and the *de facto* independence of Egypt in 1805 and subsequent Egyptian conquest of Syria in 1831-1833, the cracks in the Ottoman Empire became evident. While reforms were enacted, they failed to reverse the decline. In particular, although one of the goals of these reforms was to provide equality to non-Muslims – under a policy of "Ottomanism", the minority *millets* viewed the reforms as a loss of autonomy more than a gain of equality.

Although the empire retained nominal control, many of its provinces became independent or occupied by the great powers. In particular, the Ottomans' near total reliance on [Great Britain](Great Britain.md) and [France](France.md) to maintain its borders was exposed in the Crimean War (1853-1856). During the course of the 19th century, further territorial losses followed, with the loss of [Bosnia-Herzegovina](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Bosnia-Herzegovina), [Albania](Albania.md), [Bulgaria](Bulgaria.md), and the rest of Greece, gradually pushing the Ottomans out of the Balkans. By 1914 the empire was limited to just Anatolia and parts of the Middle East.

The Ottoman Empire was officially dissolved following World War I in 1922. Though the Allies initially planned a quite severe dismantling, the Turkish National Movement fought back in the Turkish War for Independence and was able to secure a more favorable treaty, with all of its core Anatolian territory that makes up the modern state of Turkey. The dissolution of the Ottoman Empire was followed shortly after by the dissolution of the Ottoman Caliphate in 1924. Since then, no state or individual has made a compelling claim to the title of caliph.

## Formation

If the Ottoman Empire ceases to exist, it can be [released](Releasable countries.md) by any country that controls Eastern Thrace and four other Turkish homelands, which make up its core states. All Turkish homelands except Aleppo and Crete are owned partly or entirely by the Ottoman Empire at game start.

## Scripted content

Main article:
Turkish content
The Ottoman Empire starts with two negative modifiers.

- *Sick Man of Europe*: **−33%** Prestige, **+100%** Intelligentsia political strength, **+5** Intelligentsia approval, **+50%** Modernization Movement pop attraction
- *Outmoded Bureaucracy*: **−25%** Bureaucracy and taxation capacity, **+25%** tax waste

One way to remove them[1] is for the player to accomplish certain reforms – *Tanzimats* – within 30 years. If the player fails, the event *Dead Man of Europe* happens, which inflicts numerous debuffs: it removes the Ottomans' recognition status, making it an unrecognized major power, adds a negative modifier, disbands the power bloc, removes the Rumelian tradition from European Muslim cultures, increases attraction to cultural minority movement also releases all recognized Ottoman subjects.

### Interest groups

The Ottoman Empire starts with Intelligentsia in government. The Local Governors, Armed Forces, and Sunni Ulema are in opposition. The Rural Folk, Petite Bourgeoisie, Industrialists and Trade Unions are marginalized.

The Local Governors have the Pro-Slavery ideology due to the starting Slave Trade. The Petite Bourgeoisie have the Mercantile ideology due to the starting Traditionalism.

### Diplomacy

The Ottoman Empire controls [Tripolitania](Tripolitania.md) as a puppet, and has protectorates in [Egypt](Egypt.md), [Serbia](Serbia.md), [Moldavia](Moldavia.md), [Wallachia](Wallachia.md), and [Tunis](Tunis.md). The Ottoman's Balkan protectorates have poor relations and are rebellious.

The Ottoman Empire has mutual truces with [Greece](Greece.md) until 1837 and with [Egypt](Egypt.md) until 1840. It has claims on the Egyptian states in Arabia. The Ottoman Empire and Russia have a mutual rivalry.

The Ottoman Empire's starting interest tiers are hegemonic (level 5) in Near East; pervasive (level 4) in Balkans and Nile Basin; influential (level 3) in North Africa; engaged (level 2) in Central Europe, Eastern Europe, and Southern Europe; and observant (level 1) in Arabia, Russia, and Siberia.

The Ottoman Empire leads the *Devlet-i ‘Aliye* power bloc. All members are subjects of the Ottomans. It starts with the principles Vassalization I and External Trade I.

The Ottoman Empire starts with pro-country lobbies towards [Great Britain](Great Britain.md) () and [France](France.md) (), and an anti-country lobby towards [Russia](Russia.md) ().

### Industry and resources

**Industry**

The Ottoman Empire starts with a small industrial base, primarily consumer goods. It has a large number of plantations, including a few Silk Plantations.

**Resources**

The Ottoman Empire has access to large deposits of iron, coal, and lead, and a moderate amount of sulfur, though only the iron is exploited at the game start. The Ottomans can grow several cash crops, including silk and opium.

## Strategy and guides

| ![Ambox outdated info.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/6/65/Ambox_outdated_info.png/22px-Ambox_outdated_info.png) | Please help with verifying or updating this guide. It was last verified for version 1.9. |
|---|---|

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Ottoman Empire
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

### The Tanzimat reforms

This is the outstanding objective when playing the Ottomans. You have 30 years to complete 4 of 7 objectives. Otherwise, the Tanzimat reforms will fail and trigger the JE *Dead Man of Europe*, where the nation's current leader dies, all subjects become independent, and the Ottomans become an unrecognized power. Of the reform objectives, these are likely to be fulfilled as a side-effect of average development:

- Bureaucratic Reform Change Bureaucracy and Taxation laws and have a bureaucracy surplus will almost certainly be fulfilled in passing, if the player is intending to become more wealthy,[2] and
- Suppress Separatism Have 15 years with no secession movement over 50% progress is quite likely to be fulfilled in passing; modernizing tolerance in an empire is likely to reduce radicals (and therefore turmoil). In worst case scenarios, only perform the most radical reforms either within the first 4 years, or after 15 years (if this entry is already completed, generating radicals/state turmoil won't matter!), and when turmoil is high, placate the people with low taxes, use policing and/or violent suppression, and considering easing up on the pace of reform until the people are reasonably settled. Also, consider making protectorates instead of annexing states directly while this JE is active.

The remaining 4 require some player direction to steer into; broadly speaking, there are 2 military-leaning (with one military goal having 2 stages) and 2 introspective goals.

- Of the military goals, Army Modernization Research Napoleonic Warfare, have at least 100 battalions, all barracks use General Training or better with no shortages, and have less than 25% of armies contain Irregular Infantry and Reclaim Syria (and Egypt) Own all of Adana, Aleppo, Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, and Transjordan (claims for these states are given); Reclaim Egypt is unlocked if Reclaim Syria is completed have an obvious synergy. The biggest issue is that another Power can intervene and foil the player's militaristic plans; Great Powers get a notification when the Ottomans try to reclaim Syria, and get an option to have a strategic interest in the area. Since a truce lasts 5 years and the Ottomans start the game with a truce with Egypt (expires in 1840*), there would only be 5 opportunities to wage war... but there may be ways to wage more than 5 wars against them (discussed further down). Be aware that General Staff unlocks Skirmisher Infantry, which may trigger shortages in ammunition if adopted. In any case, the Ottomans' starting economy is not strong enough to support upgrading all its irregulars to line infantry.
- The "introspective" goals of Urbanization Incorporate and have at least 1 Urban Center building in at least 75% of all owned states and Educational Reform Raise literacy by 20% and have at least a level 5 university (fully staffed) are likely better for a long-term plan of modernizing the Ottoman Empire, as they increase productivity via industry and research capability, but will require foresight and urban planning. That being said, they are still worth pursuing, as their long-term benefits will be considerable, especially once the Ottoman economy begins to grow and take off.

As a nation considered to be falling behind, the limited resources the player has must be carefully allocated, and attempting to chase all the goals at once may lead to the player getting none. As a saying goes: "The man who chases two rabbits catches neither"; be careful that you do not over-extend. Play methodically, and try to concentrate your efforts at one task at a time, while trying to stay within the 30-year deadline.

### Early civil war

Switching sides and playing as a successful revolution will remove the truce with Egypt (and Greece), reset Ottoman diplomatic relations with almost every GP (this includes removing the rivalry and low relations with Russia) and remove all negative modifiers (temporarily for some, others like Bankruptcy are cleared), consider setting a civil war in motion from day one. At the various checkpoints (if possible), take the option to add other IGs to the revolutionary cause, increasing the ease of the subsequent civil war. Remember to switch to the other side when the Diplomatic Play starts. Whichever way you go about starting this revolution, make sure to keep the Monarchy, as a large part of the Ottoman Empire's flavour is preserving its ancient monarchy (the Osman dynasty having reigned since 1299) and reforming it to suit the needs and conditions of the modern world.

### The wars with Egypt

It might be tempting to declare war on Egypt as soon as the [truce](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Truce) expires. But the Ottoman Empire's growth potential is much greater than Egypt's, so the longer one waits, the more one-sided the war will be. However, once war breaks out one should aim to take the states of Upper Egypt and Lower Egypt on top of the Ottoman claims. Note that if the player causes a civil war, switches over to the opposing side and wins the civil war, the truce will be cancelled. This means that a successful revolution (usually) allows the Ottomans to attack Egypt earlier. A successful civil war also removes the negative prestige modifier, allowing the Ottoman Empire to remain a Great Power comfortably, and thus extra maneuvers to make all war goals primary demands (at the cost of infamy generation from being a Great Power)

The Ottoman fleet is much superior to the Egyptians'. The war is best fought by simply holding both fronts (Levant and Tripolitania), while preparing a [naval invasion](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Naval_invasion) in Upper Egypt. Once a foothold is established there, the Egyptian war effort will be thrown into disarray as they redirect armies and weaken their frontlines, allowing one to begin advancing the aforementioned fronts.

Remember that at least two wars are needed to complete both Conquest journals which are related to Egyptian conquests. With Egypt as a protectorate, it is crucial that a diplomatic play is not triggered when reducing it to a puppet. Once Egypt is a puppet, the Ottomans can then annex all of it in a single war. To reduce liberty desire, consider conquering states and gifting them to Egypt, but be sure not to increase Egypt's prestige by too much.

### Discrimination and Ottomanism

The Tanzimats do not touch on this, but as the Ottoman Empire is home to a multitude of cultures and religions, it is prudent to adopt *Freedom of Conscience* or even *Total Separation* to increase [acceptance](Acceptance.md) and also *Multiculturalism* to again increase acceptance. However, after patch 1.10, the Ottomans' unique Millet System law replacing State Religion does increase acceptance for Shiite Muslims and followers of other Abrahamic Religions (Christianity and Judaism).

The reasoning for such a radical reform is that, as long as a pop is being discriminated against, it will *NOT* assimilate (but will convert religion), and you will also risk generating Discrimination radicals. Fulfilling the demands of the Tanzimats is by no means easy to achieve, and yet, it is still not enough. The Ottoman Empire in 1836 is a large multi-ethnic, multi-religious state covering a large area in Europe and Asia, and once you begin expanding to reconquer lost territory such as Egypt, that will only become more apparent. You must reform the empire in a more tolerant and accommodating direction, as otherwise unhappy pops and radicalism will tear the realm asunder. While the game never touches on this, enacting *Total Separation* and *Multiculturalism* is actually more historical than it might seem, as it simulates the concept of [Ottomanism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/en:Ottomanism)[wp 1] quite well.

Given the difficulty in passing *Multiculturalism*, a possible alternative is to have subjects with European primary cultures administering states which are not Turkish homelands. This means reducing the autonomy of [Serbia](Serbia.md) and the Romanian states, and giving Albanian and Greek states (and other European homelands) to them.

### Viable economies

The Ottoman Empire begins with no industry to speak of, but has a lot of of states with the potential for [cotton](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Cotton_Plantation), [tobacco](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Tobacco_Plantation) and [tea plantations](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Tea_plantations). Building those and exporting tea to the [United Kingdom](Great Britain.md) is a very profitable plan for early-game income due to the low [construction](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Construction) costs and the British [obsession](Vickypedia.md) for tea. Expanding the construction sector early is very important, as that will allow you to build more buildings, which are sorely needed. You will also need to build logging camps to sustain them and then also more Tooling Workshops to upgrade their production methods.

Speaking of resources, the Ottoman Empire does not have sufficient iron and coal for full industrialization compared to its size, which means that once those resources are fully utilised, more will have to be either imported, or secured through conquest. The Ottomans also do not start with dye; the nearest sources are in Persia and the state of Upper Egypt. On a lesser note, coffee is also not available.

Always try to hold onto your territories in the Balkans and if possible, expand westwards to seize more land from Austria, as the region contains a large amount of coal and smaller amounts of wood, iron and lead. Several of the states also have the Terra Rossa trait, increasing agriculture throughput. The total amount of arable land is not very much, but it helps. Should you desire to acquire more arable land, then you could try to expand northward into the Ukraine regions, where you will find states with the Black Soil trait. But beware, as that will require picking and winning a fight with Russia, which is only likely to become possible later on, once the economy and military have been brought up to par. Expanding in Europe also means discriminated pops which may be a permanent problem until Multiculturalism is passed, and 25 years for incorporating states should you choose to do so.

Do not forget the *Mesopotamian Floodplain* exists and is another area for some agriculture.

When expanding westwards in North Africa, consider grabbing both Spanish Al-Rif and Gibraltar for the lucrative Gibraltar toll. Unlike other tolls, the Gibraltar toll can only be collected when one power controls *both* ends.

### The Russian bear

The Ottomans have the relative bad luck of having Russia as a neighbour. With bad relations, land access and a strong army, Russia intervening in Ottoman wars is always bad news. Morever, other Great Powers are unlikely to effectively help the Ottomans; [Prussia](Prussia.md) and [Austria](Austria.md) both start with a defence pact with Russia. Consider releasing [Bulgaria](Bulgaria.md) and [Syria](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Syria) (or [Kurdistan](Kurdistan.md) after researching *Nationalism*) as buffer states (puppets with service exemptions). For Syria, use treaties to transfer Diyarbakir, Erzurum and Kars. Note that Wallachia and Moldavia start with *Treaty of Adrianople*, which grants military access to Russia.

### Advance in the Middle East

Once Egypt has been reconquered, [Persia](Persia.md) makes for a very tempting and quite lucrative mid-game target for Ottoman expansion, and is a logical next step, especially given the above resource problem. Conquering Persia will give you access to a decent amount of coal, iron and lead as well as more discoverable oil, to fuel further industrialisation and production. Additionally, several of the Persian states have the ability to support [opium](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Opium_Plantation) and [silk plantations](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Silk_Plantation). Just make sure that you have adopted at least *Freedom of Conscience*, and also other laws that lessen discrimination against pops of foreign cultures and religions, as otherwise you will also be introducing a large foreign population into your realm capable of spawning plenty of radicals.

### The Romanian Question

[Wallachia](Wallachia.md) and [Moldavia](Moldavia.md) are involved in the unification of [Romania](Romania.md), and the Romanian Question is also of concern to Austria and Russia. It is up to the player if Romania should be left alone, to be contested between Austria and Russia, or be annexed and become part of the empire.

If Wallachia is annexed, the Steaua Română company can be established, which can be combined with the vast oil outputs of Arabia to make the Ottomans produce this crucial resource in enormous amounts.

### Strategies against the Ottomans

As the *Sick Man of Europe*, the Ottoman Empire has many valuable protectorates; examples include [Egypt](Egypt.md), [Moldavia](Moldavia.md), and [Wallachia](Wallachia.md). Look out for opportunities to intervene in diplomatic plays where the Ottomans are willing to offer themselves as a protectorate (once they have become unrecognized) or transfer a subject. Once the Ottomans become a protectorate, reducing them to a puppet will result in taking over all their subjects.
