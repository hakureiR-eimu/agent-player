The military overview window (shortcut F5) has tabs for the army, mobilization, and navy. These tabs show formation summaries and an overview of mobilization options. Orders can be given to each formation from these tabs.

In Victoria 3, all wars start as a result of a [diplomatic play](Diplomatic play.md) in which neither side backs down. Even before actual war has broken out, it is possible to mobilize and deploy military forces during the diplomatic play, which is important both in projecting strength during the play (increasing the chance of the other side backing down without a war) and winning the war if it does break out.

## Formations

A country's military is organized into *formations*: armies and fleets. Each formation contains a number of units and can be led by only one commander. New units and commanders are recruited through formations. Each formation has a home HQ – strategic region – which can be changed at any time except for mobilized armies. Additionally, formations can be stationed at other HQs. The primary distinction between a home HQ and stationing at another HQ comes from the **−50%** attrition reduction formations receive while stationed in their home HQ.

Land formations – *armies* – can be stationed either at an HQ or a front. At a front, the army can either advance or defend. Armies must be mobilized to be stationed at a front and participate in any battles. An army without any generals can still defend a front, though only up to 10 battalions before gaining penalties.

Naval formations – *fleets* – can be stationed either at an HQ or a sea node. Fleets stationed at an HQ do not participate in any battles. At a sea node, Fleets can intercept, raid convoys, escort convoys, or blockade. Fleets are always mobilized and always require an admiral to be deployed to a sea node.

### Organization

Formations have an organization value, from 0% to 100%, though typically not below 25%. Organization is reduced by changing [mobilization options](Land warfare.md) or from a reduced maximum organization. Maximum organization is reduced when a formation does not have enough command limit, has more than 50% special units, or has supply penalty. Low organization reduces the effectiveness of military units. The penalty scales from nothing at **100%** organization to the following full penalty at **0%** organization:

- **−75%** Offense
- **−75%** Defense
- **−75%** Blockade strength
- **−100%** Morale recovery
- **−50%** Recovery rate
- **−75%** Convoy raiding efficiency
- **−75%** Convoy escort defense

While a formation is not travelling, if its organization is below the maximum, it increases by **1%** daily (per /Victoria 3/game/common/static_modifiers/00_code_static_modifiers.txt's base_values, "100 days to get max organization" — double the previously-documented 0.5%), reduced if over the command limit. The technology Military Statistics still increases organization gain by **+20%** – to **1.2%** daily at base.

If organization is above the maximum, each day it loses 5% of the current value or 1, whichever is higher.

Exiled formations have 0 maximum organization.

## Military units

See also:
Military buildings
Military units in Victoria 3 are divided into land units – Battalions – generated by Barracks and Conscription Centers, and naval units – Flotillas – generated by Naval Bases. Battalions are further divided into *regulars*, standing units, and *conscripts*, units called up only for war.

Each unit has a *manpower* value related to its building's employment per level, with a maximum of 1000. It also has a *morale* percent value that recovers after battle, up to the formation's supply level. Its other stats, such as offense and defense, depend primarily on the unit's type. Units are automatically assigned to the formation they are recruited to and can be transferred to other formations at any time except while in battle.

When regulars and flotillas are added to a formation, their Barracks or Naval Bases are added to the construction queue and built normally like any other building. When conscripts are added to an army, the Conscription Centers are immediately added to the state. Each building level employs 1000 individuals, split between Servicemen and Officers; with full employment, the building produces one unit per level. The time to hire individuals is the *training rate*, which increases with more advanced *Training* and *Naval Theory* production methods.

### Unit types

See also:
Land units
,
Naval units
Battalions and flotillas are each divided into four groups now (was three for each as of 1.9). Battalions can be *Infantry*, *Artillery*, *Cavalry*, or *Marines* (Marines are new — exempt from the naval invasion efficiency penalty other units suffer). These are further divided into types that are unlocked by [military technology](Military technology.md). Similarly, flotillas can be *Capital Ships*, *Cruisers*, *Torpedo Craft*, or *Supply Ships* — not "Light Ships"/"Capital Ships"/"Support Vessels" as previously documented, matching the current /Victoria 3/game/common/ship_groups names; again further divided into types.

Each unit's base stats depend on its type. Often, a unit type can be upgraded into a more advanced type. Upgrading a unit demobilizes it, and then – if the formation is mobilized – remobilizes it. Only a certain percentage of a mobilized formation's units can be upgraded each week, **10%** for armies and **5%** for fleets. An upgraded unit loses one veterancy level and current experience (a Veteran unit with 300/400 xp before upgrade, will become Seasoned with 0/200 xp after upgrade).

### Prestige goods

Units which use prestige goods gain a bonus relative to the input share of prestige goods. At 100% prestige goods input share, units gain the following bonus:

- **+15%** Offense
- **+15%** Defense
- **+25%** Morale recovery

This scales down with percentage of usage. A unit with 50% input share of prestige goods would receive 50% of the above effects.

### Power projection

| Power projection prestige |  |
|---|---|
| Army | 0.03 |
| Navy | **0.006** (was 0.1) |
| Subject army | 0.0005 |
| Subject navy | 0.01 |

Power projection represents how impressive a country's military is. For **armies** it's still calculated as the average of each unit's offense and defense times their manpower ratio (divisor 1000). For **navies** this changed along with the ship stat rework — offense/defense no longer exist for ships, so ship power projection is now computed from **0.33**× hull damage + **0.33**× crew damage + **0.33**× armor + **0.1**× max hit points instead. The navy prestige-per-point multiplier was cut from 0.1 to **0.006** accordingly (ship stat values are much larger under the new schema, e.g. hit points alone run into the thousands, so the per-point rate had to shrink to keep total prestige output comparable) — the army-side prestige rate (0.03) and both subject rates are unchanged. The sum of all units' power projection gives the country's total power projection. This provides various benefits; most obviously, prestige and declared interests. Prestige is gained as on the right; the "100 naval power projection per additional interest" threshold could not be independently verified against the new ship power projection scale.

Newly hired commanders begin at rank 1, and can be promoted at any time, with a sufficient bureaucracy surplus available. ## Fronts and theaters

Clicking on a front brings up a detailed view of the front, showing the armies on each side and the orders they have been given.
Once a country is engaged in a diplomatic play or war, *fronts* appear at each border between allied and enemy territory. A front is created for each pair of *theaters* − contiguous territory controlled by one side of the conflict.

An "interruption" in the border such as a sea or neutral country, does not count if both sides continue around the interruption in a single country, as for example [Russia](Russia.md) and the [Ottoman Empire](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Turkey) around the Black Sea. If the interruption divides different countries on the same side, such as might happen with [Brazil](Brazil.md) fighting against [Bolivia](Bolivia.md) and [Argentina](Argentina.md), then separate fronts are created. Potentially separate fronts are joined together, however, if there is only one state region separating them, even if this creates a joined front on opposite sides of the same state.

Fronts advance state-by-state. Each time a battle is won, the winning side gains *occupation* score. This score takes control of a percentage of a state along the front. When a state is fully occupied by one side, the front moves to incorporate that state. When this happens, fronts may merge, split or, disappear altogether. If a front ceases to exist, armies assigned to that front automatically travel to another front or return to an HQ, usually the nearest one.

## Invasion

See also:
Naval invasions
Land invasions require an army and military access through every neutral country along the way to be performed. It cannot be initiated if it takes more than 30 days for the army to travel from its current station to the destination (for long distances, it may first prove necessary to mobilize the army in order to benefit from any potential speed bonuses).

Once an invasion is planned, a front is created at the targeted state and both the defender and the attacker can assign additional armies to this front (the 30 days travel time rule does not apply anymore). Defender's armies stationed in the region HQ **do not participate** in the defense of a land invasion. Attacking armies assigned to a invasion cannot be removed, nor their battalions detached, until the invasion has ended or is canceled.

It takes two weeks for battles to begin once the first invading army arrives, this gives the defending side a chance to bring an army to the invasion.

If the attacker loses three battles while having 0% occupation of the targeted state's territory, the invasion is defeated and the invading army must return to a friendly HQ. Any defending armies will also return to their nearest friendly HQ.

One the targeted state is fully occupied, the invasion is successful and a new regular front is created.

naval invasion requires both a fleet and one or two armies. The armies can either be an army of marines embarked on the fleet, a regular army transported for the occasion, or both. Naval invasions cannot be initiated if armies has less power projection than the naval fortification invasion defense of the state.

Once the invasion is confirmed, if a regular army has been selected, the fleet first regroups with it on a friendly coastal state and then travels to the sea node adjacent to the targeted state. For the invasion to proceed, the sea node must be uncontested to establish a beachhead. The sea node is *contested* if any defending fleet of at least 25% of the attacker fleet's power projection is deployed there. Once the army has landed, contesting the sea has no effect.

It takes two weeks for battles to begin. Defender's armies stationed in the region HQ **do participate** in the defense of a naval invasion. However, in case of multiple invasions, it is better to assign armies specifically to each one, as a stationed army could commit all its battalions to a single battle and leave the other invasion uncovered.

There is a *Naval Invasion Efficiency* penalty of **−25%** to attack for every invading battalions that is not of Marines type.

The naval invasion fails if the invading fleet loses any naval battle or if the army loses three battles while having 0% occupation of the targeted state's territory. Then the army and fleet travel back to their home HQ.

Once the targeted state is fully occupied, the invasion is successful and a new regular front is created.

### Naval Fortification

[Naval Fortifications](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Naval_Fortification) fortify a coastal state against naval assaults and naval invasions. They provide resistance against naval invasions, blockades, and bombardments.

- Advance orders direct the general to attempt to occupy enemy-held territory; while no battle is occurring on the front, progress towards the next advance accumulates daily. When 100 progress is reached, the army engages any opposing army or advances occupation if there is no opposition. If both sides are advancing, this typically results in alternating attacks. Generals with Advance orders still defend against advancing enemy armies.
- Defend orders direct the general to prevent enemy advances. Generals ordered to defend can recapture friendly territory, but never occupy enemy territory.

Orders have no effects if the general's army is not assigned to a front.

## Mobilization

See also:
Mobilization options
It is possible to mobilize armies all at once, or one at a time. Mobilizing armies individually provides an estimate of the cost to the budget for the army's mobilization options, but does not estimate the effect of increased base upkeep.
In order to give a army an order, it must be mobilized. Mobilization takes between 5 and 20 days depending on state infrastructure and other factors.

In contrast to armies, fleets are always active and do not require mobilization.

### Conscription

Similarly to mobilization, *conscript* battalions can be activated army-by-army during a diplomatic play or war. Conscripts are hired when activated, rather than being employed as a standing unit, and take some time to fully mobilize. Once activated, conscripts cannot return to civilian life while mobilized. The number of conscripts available depends primarily on a country's [Army Model laws](Power structure laws.md) and its states' population. Many factors affect the conscription rate, such as unincorporated states having **−50%** conscription.

### Demobilization

Armies can be manually demobilized at any time. Demobilizing an army this way incurs a 90 decaying expense equal to the mobilization cost of that army's units, and the army cannot be remobilized until after 90 days have passed. All armies are demobilized for free when a country is at peace and not involved in any diplomatic plays. A demobilized army automatically returns to its home HQ.

## Supply

Early in this war between Britain and Spain, the losses to attrition far outstrip those to battle.
Formations fighting overseas rely on the [supply network](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Supply_network) to keep them equipped and in supply. If enemy fleets raid the supply network, sinking convoys, this impacts on the supply of formations, which has a direct impact on the morale recovery of units. For example, if raiding has reduced a supply network's efficiency by 2%, then the morale recovery of units overseas is 98%. If supply is reduced beyond 95%, it applies an equal penalty to organization. For example, a unit with 80% supply would have a 20% penalty to maximum organization.

Additionally, [shortages](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Shortage) for units' buildings reduce a formations supply to the same result.

### Attrition

All mobilized formations are subject to attrition, which leads to both wounded and killed pops. The impact of this can be substantial and can outstrip the number of wounded and killed from battle. Formations receive **−50%** less attrition while stationed at their home HQ.

Base attrition is **10%** and casualties from attrition can range from **4%** to **12%** weekly.

Battalions actively engaged in a battle temporarily do not suffer attrition.

## Combat stats

If you have knowledge of this topic please verify the information, otherwise discuss it in the talk page . |
|---|---|

Both land and naval units use a series of stats to affect combat. These stats are determined primarily by the unit's type, but are added or modified by many factors, such as commander orders, technologies, and more.

The primary combat stat is *offense* or *defense*. Units in the attacking formation use offense while units in the defending formation use defense. These stats increase the number of casualties and morale damage inflicted by units in combat.

*Morale* represents what percentage of a unit's manpower is able to continue in combat. Each unit has a base amount of *morale loss*, which reduces the units morale each tick in battle, in addition to morale damage inflicted by enemy units. While not in battle, morale recovers slowly each day, at a base of 3% modified by *morale recovery*.

*Manpower* represents the individuals that make up a unit. Each unit has a maximum of 1000 manpower, which directly corresponds to the pops employed in the building that generated the unit. *Casualties* are losses to a units manpower either from *attrition* or combat. Combat casualties are either made into a dependent or killed, with the chance of death between 0.1% to 0.5%. The death chance is increased by enemy's *kill rate* and reduced by a units *recovery rate*. There is a minimum of 5 casualties per unit per tick, and officers receive 50% fewer casualties than servicemen.

In this war, France is on the back foot, primarily because its opponent controls 50% of its war goals. Control of war goals is often critical to a successful war.
France has decided to cut its losses and negotiate a white peace, rather than drag out a costly war. This was only possible because Adagh’s war support was also low.
When a war begins, each participating country has **+100** *war support*. Each week, each country loses an amount of war support due to *exhaustion* depending on various factors, such as war casualties, occupation percentage, and percentage of radical pops.

| Exhaustion | Cause |
|---|---|
| **+0.2** | Base gain |
| **+1** | Enemy controls all war goals (scaled by percentage) |
| **+0.015** | Per 1% of population that are radicals |
| **+0.1** | Per 10% (scaling) casualties compared to max military manpower with no battles lost |
| **+0.4** | Per 10% (scaling) casualties compared to max military manpower with all battles lost |
| **+0.1** | Per 10% cultural casualties on both sides of war[2] |
| **+0.01** | Per percent of clout of lobbies opposed to the war |
| **+0.01** | Per percent of clout of lobbies supporting the war |
|  | Occupation percentage (highest only) |
| **+0.1** | Any (>0%) |
| **+0.5** | Above 10% |
| **+1.0** | Above 25% |
| **+2.0** | Above 50% |
| **+3.0** | Above 75% |
| **+10** | Above 90% |

A country cannot go below **0** war support unless an enemy occupies all war goals or the country's capital state. Countries that are not targeted by a wargoal do not have this block, and automatically tick below 0 as expected. Wargoals against a subject country are counted as targeting the overlord.

### Capitulation

Once an independent country reaches **−100** war support, it automatically capitulates. All war goals against it are enforced and it leaves the war. A country can also choose to voluntarily capitulate at any time during the war. Subjects cannot choose to capitulate and do not automatically capitulate unless their overlord capitulates.

When a country capitulates, the war continues if any war goals remain. If all countries on one side of a war are fully occupied, all those countries capitulate immediately.

### Peace negotiation

Aside from capitulation, it is also possible to negotiate peace by selecting [war goals](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/War_goals) from either side to press or not press. Each independent country with or targeted by a war goal – the *negotiating participants* – must agree to the peace deal, making it difficult to leave an ally in the lurch for their support.

Countries without and not targeted by any war goals, as well as subjects, have no say on the peace deal.

It is not possible to negotiate a peace deal with only some enemy countries; any negotiated peace deal ends the entire war.
