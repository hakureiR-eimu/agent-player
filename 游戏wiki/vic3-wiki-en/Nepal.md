*"Almost all of India is now under the direct or indirect control of the British East India Company. The Nawabs of the princely states still exert a strong degree of rule over their territories, but ultimately answer to the Company. Will India ever be free from British rule?"*

**Nepal** is an unrecognized regional power in the northern India. Being sandwiched between [East India](East India.md) and [China](China.md), expansion is very difficult.

## Formation

If it ceases to exist, Nepal can be [released](Releasable countries.md) by any country controlling its capital and only core state of Western Himalayas.

### Interest groups

Nepal begins with the Landowners in government. The Hindu Priesthood, Kisanharu, Armed Forces, and Intelligentsia are in opposition, while the Petite Bourgeoisie, Trade Unions, and Industrialists are marginalized.

The Hindu Priesthood has the Hindu Moralist ideology instead of the default Moralist. The Petite Bourgeoisie has the Modernizer ideology instead of the default Reactionary. The Intelligentsia has the Constitutionalist ideology instead of the default Republican.

## Strategies and guides

The below is
one of many
player suggested strategies for
Nepal
. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.
Nepal begins as a small, landlocked nation nestled in the Himalayas, squeezed between the vast [Qing Empire](China.md) and the expanding [British East India Company](East India.md). Starting with limited territory and population, Nepal's path to regional dominance hinges on exploiting windows of great power instability — particularly in China — to rapidly expand and eventually challenge British hegemony over the subcontinent.

### Early game expansion

The opening years are critical and largely deterministic. Immediately annex [Sikkim](Sikkim.md), followed shortly by [Bhutan](Bhutan.md), ideally within the first 1–2 years. These conquests are essential for strengthening Nepal's strategic position and are effectively mandatory for long-term success.

After consolidating the eastern Himalayas, shift focus toward [Tibet](Tibet.md). A war against Tibet should only be initiated when external pressure weakens [Great Qing](China.md), most commonly during:

- A Qing civil war, or
- The outbreak of the Opium Wars with [Great Britain](Great Britain.md)

These situations create a window in which Qing support for Tibet is limited, allowing Nepal to press a Casus Belli safely.

To prepare, rush the Mandatory Service technology to unlock the Enlistment Efforts decree, and maximize available conscript battalions. Mobilize your forces, but do not immediately deploy to the Himalayan front. Qing and Tibetan troops are often distracted by internal conflicts, allowing Nepal to rapidly occupy Tibetan territory with minimal resistance.

Once the two Tibetan states are fully occupied, switch your armies to a defensive stance and stall until Qing capitulates. Tibet can then be annexed and its homelands incorporated, providing a significant boost to population and resources.

### Consolidating western China

If favorable RNG leads to the fragmentation of [China](China.md), act immediately. Newly released states provide an opportunity to consolidate Tibetan homelands, which is one of the most efficient ways for Nepal to grow an incorporated population.

At this stage, increased migration to the Himalayas is common and highly beneficial, as labor is extremely valuable during early industrialization.

Priority conquests should include:

- Qinghai from [Uyghuristan](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Uyghuristan)
- Sichuan from the [Sichuan](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Sichuan) warlord state

To secure a coastal province, Nepal has two primary options:

1. Conquer Yunnan from the [Yunnan](Yunnan.md) warlord state, then take Tonkin from [Dai Nam](Dai Nam.md)
2. Expand westward toward the [Khalsa Raj](Punjab.md) (see below)

### Without Chinese fragmentation

If China does not fracture due to unfavorable RNG, Nepal must pursue a more diplomatic and military-intensive path. Begin by improving relations with a great power, typically [Russia](Russia.md) or [Great Britain](Great Britain.md), to secure support in a war against the [Khalsa Raj](Punjab.md).

The primary objective is the annexation of [Kashmir](Kashmir.md), which incorporates relatively quickly (approximately 5 years). If Kashmir alone is insufficient, further expansion may be required:

- Annex [Chitral](Chitral.md)
- Wage war against Afghan states to seize Pashtunistan, Quetta, and Baluchistan
- Launch Annex State casus belli on [Sindh](Sindh.md) to secure a port

While this approach is significantly slower and more tedious, it is the only viable alternative to the Tonkin route for securing a port.

### Challenging the British Empire

Once Nepal reaches a population of approximately 30–40 million and achieves a GDP per capita of 0.5 or higher, it becomes viable to directly challenge [Great Britain](Great Britain.md).

With 200–300 battalions (standing or conscriptable), Nepal can initiate a [diplomatic play](Diplomatic play.md) to liberate [East India](East India.md). [Russia](Russia.md) may join the play if favorable RNG occurs, and combined participation dramatically increases the likelihood of success.

### Post-liberation expansion

Following independence, [East India](East India.md) typically fractures and is inherited as [Bengal](Bengal.md). It is crucial that Nepal does **not** maintain a [treaty](Treaty.md) with Bengal at this stage.

Immediately target either:

- Bengal, or
- Delhi

These regions provide an easily incorporated tax base and valuable raw materials. [Bengal](Bengal.md) is often heavily indebted or near default, creating an ideal opportunity for annexation.

Successfully executing this step allows Nepal to emerge as a major power without further large-scale mobilization. From this point onward, standard mid- and late-game strategies can be employed, enabling Nepal to develop into either an economic powerhouse or a global hegemon capable of asserting dominance on the world stage.
